## Basliklar + ozetler

- **The Boy and the Filberts** (classic): A hungry child grabs too many tasty treats and gets stuck.
- **The Two Pots** (classic): Two different pots near a river must decide whether to enter the water together.
- **Androcles and the Lion** (classic): A poor man named Androcles helps a wounded lion and they become friends.
- **The Monkey's Heart** (classic): A small person by the water meets a powerful stranger who asks for his life.
- **The Talkative Tortoise** (classic): When the water dries up, two tiny friends must help their old turtle friend.
- **The Brahmin and the Pots (Castle in Air)** (classic): A poor man saves food and dreams of a rich future, but his big dreams cause trouble.
- **The Crane and the Crab** (classic): An old bird by the water struggles for food and seeks help from a small friend.
- **The Wise Little Girl** (classic): A kind father and his clever child must answer three riddles for a powerful leader.

### The Talkative Tortoise (B1)

Long ago, a very happy animal lived near water with two bird friends. They spent much time together every day. Then, a bad summer came to the land. The water dried up completely because of the hot sun. The birds decided to leave that home for a new place. If they stay, they will find no water. The animal felt very sad because he did not want to stay behind alone. He has lived by this water for many years. Now, the summer has changed everything around them. The bird friends wanted to help him, so they made a plan. They brought a long stick. The animal would hold the stick with his mouth. Each bird would take one side of the stick in its face to fly up high. If the animal holds the stick well, the trip will be safe. The animal was warned that he must never open his mouth during the trip. He promised to keep quiet. Soon, the three friends rose high into the sky above the green land. People down on the ground looked up at them with great surprise and spoke. They wondered what strange thing was being carried by the birds in the air. The animal heard these comments and felt a strong urge to answer the crowd. He has never seen so many people in one place before. If he speaks now, he will fall down. He forgot the warning completely and opened his mouth to talk to the people. As soon as his mouth let go of the stick, he began to fall down fast. The poor animal hit the hard ground because he could not control his speech. If he had kept his mouth closed, he would have reached the new home safely. This simple story shows why people should always keep quiet when danger is near. The animal has lived many years near the water. The birds have known him for a long time. They have always tried to be good friends. When the summer came, everything became dry. The animal has lost his home now. The birds have made a clever plan. They have found a good stick for the journey. The animal has bitten the middle part of the stick. Each bird has taken one end in its face. They have flown high into the sky. Many people have looked up at them. The animal has heard the noise below. He has forgotten the warning from his friends. He has opened his mouth to speak. He has fallen down to the ground. If he stays quiet, he will not fall. If the summer had not been so hot, they would have stayed.

### The Boy and the Filberts (B1)

Once upon a time, a young boy walked near a large garden where a house stood. Inside that house, a nice glass container was placed upon a brown table. The container was completely filled with sweet nuts. The young boy looked inside the room and saw the open container. Because he wanted something good to eat, he walked inside without any fear. He put his hand deep into the narrow neck of the glass container. His fingers held as many food pieces as he could hold in his hand. He tried to pull his hand back out with all the food. However, his closed hand was now too big for the narrow opening. He could not pull his hand out because the hole was too small. If he had taken only a few pieces, his hand would have slipped back out. Instead, he held onto every single piece that he had held. The poor boy stood there by the table, feeling very sad and confused. He began to cry a little bit because his hand was stuck fast. His desire for too much had caused a funny problem which he could not solve alone.

After some time, a friend who lived in the house walked into the room. The kind friend saw the boy standing by the table with his hand caught. The good friend looked at the situation and gave him a simple piece of advice. The person told the child to let go of half the food immediately. The boy listened to this good suggestion and dropped half of the items. As soon as his hand became smaller, his arm easily came out of the container. He was finally free, and he still had enough food left to enjoy. The child learned that taking less at one time is better than losing everything. He has never forgotten this important lesson about life. If he ever sees another open container, he will remember his past mistake. His friend has also told other people about the funny event. Everyone in the house now laughs when they talk about the day. The young boy always shares his food with others now. He has shared many meals since that day. If he sees someone else in trouble, he will help them right away.

### The Crane and the Crab (B1)

Once upon a time, a big bird lived near a clear water pool. The bird loved to eat fish every single day. Growing old made catching food quite difficult. Because running fast was no longer possible, the good animal needed a good plan. The bird stood by the water and looked very sad. Soon, a little animal came close and asked why the big bird was crying. The bird told a story about danger coming to the pool. The bird said that men would soon come to take all the fish away. The small animal, which felt very worried, asked if there was any safe place. The bird pointed toward a lovely lake far over the hills and offered help.

Day after day, the bird carried fish away in its long mouth. Instead of helping them, the bird ate every single one. Soon, it was the turn of the small animal to take the trip. The small animal climbed up, and they flew high above the land. Looking down, the good animal saw many fish bones on the ground and realized the truth. Without any fear, the small creature used its strong legs to hold the bird fast. The bird could not move until it promised to bring the small animal safely back down. The bird could not breathe properly until it promised to bring the small animal safely back down. When they reached the ground again, the quick animal did not let go. The bad bird was finally punished for being so bad.

From that day, the good animal lived happily and safely near the water. The big bird wanted more food for every single meal. The old animal stayed by the clear pool day after day. Many small fish lived in the water under the sun. The bird looked at the fish and thought about lunch. The little animal watched the big bird with great care. The animal walked slowly across the ground near the pool. The bird told the animal that men would take everyone. The animal wanted to find a safe place far away. The bird offered to carry the animal to a new home.

They flew high above the green land for a long time. The animal saw many things on the ground below them. Bones of other fish showed the true plan of the bird. The animal felt angry and held the neck of the bird. The bird could not move or go down very fast. The bird promised to return to the ground right now. The animal stayed safe and lived near the water again. Many animals lived near the water in that part of the world. The pool was clear and full of life every single morning. The big bird spent hours standing by the side of the pool.

The animal came to the water and saw the sad bird. The bird spoke about men coming to take the fish away. The animal believed the story because the bird seemed good. They started the journey across the hills toward the lake. The bird stopped on the way and ate the fish. That action showed the true nature of the big bird. When the animal learned the truth, it felt no fear.
