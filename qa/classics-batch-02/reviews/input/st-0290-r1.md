# Arthur and the Sword in the Stone

Summary: When the old king dies, a sword in a stone waits for the one who can pull it out.

## Level A1

King Uther died. Nobody knew the next king. Many lords wanted the crown. Merlin was a wise old man. He went to the Archbishop of Canterbury. "Call the lords to London," said Merlin.

At Christmas, the lords came to a church. Outside, they saw a great stone. On the stone sat an anvil of steel. A sword stood deep in the anvil. There were gold words on the stone. "Pull out this sword, and be king!"

Every lord pulled and pulled. Nothing happened. "Our king is not here," said the Archbishop. Ten knights stayed and watched the stone.

Soon there was a big tournament. Sir Ector came with his son Kay. Young Arthur came too. He was like a brother to Kay. On the way, Kay stopped his horse. "I have no sword!" cried Kay. "Get my sword from the house, Arthur."

Arthur rode back to the house. Nobody opened the door. "My brother needs a sword," thought Arthur. He rode to the church. Nobody was there. Arthur pulled the sword from the stone. It came out very easily!

Arthur gave the sword to Kay. Kay knew this sword. "Father, look!" said Kay. "I have the sword from the stone!" Sir Ector took the boys to the church. "Tell me the truth, Kay," said Ector. "Arthur gave it to me," said Kay.

Arthur put the sword back in the stone. Ector and Kay pulled hard. The sword did not move. Then Arthur pulled it out again. Ector and Kay went down on their knees. "I am not your father," said Ector. "Merlin brought you to me as a baby. You are our true king." Arthur was very sad.

The lords were angry. "A boy cannot be our king!" they cried. Again and again, Arthur pulled out the sword. At Pentecost, all the people came to see. "Arthur is our king!" they shouted. The lords said sorry, and Arthur forgave them. So Arthur became the King of England.

## Level A2

King Uther Pendragon died, and Britain had no king. Many strong lords wanted the crown for themselves. Merlin, a wise man, went to the Archbishop of Canterbury. "Call all the lords to London for Christmas," said Merlin. "God can show them the true king."

At Christmas, the lords prayed in the greatest church in London. After the prayers, they saw a strange thing. A great square stone stood near the church. On top of it sat an anvil of steel. A bright sword stood deep in the anvil. There were gold letters on the stone. "Only the true king of England can pull out this sword."

One by one, the lords tried, but the sword did not move. "The true king is not here yet," said the Archbishop. Ten good knights guarded the stone day and night.

Soon there was a big tournament in London. Sir Ector rode to it with his son, Sir Kay. Kay was a new knight. Young Arthur rode with them. He grew up in Ector's home, like a brother to Kay. On the road, Kay suddenly stopped his horse. "My sword!" he cried. "I left it at our house in town. Please go back for it, Arthur."

At the house, Arthur knocked, but nobody opened the door. Everyone was out at the tournament. "My brother needs a sword today," Arthur said to himself. Then he remembered the sword in the stone. He rode to the church, and nobody was there. The guards were at the tournament too. Arthur took the sword by the handle and pulled gently. It came out of the stone very easily.

Arthur rode fast to Kay and gave him the sword. Kay knew it at once, and he showed it to his father. "Look, Father, this is the sword from the stone!" said Kay. "So I must be the king!" Ector rode back to the church with both boys. There he asked Kay to swear on the holy book. "How did you get this sword?" asked Ector. "Arthur brought it to me," said Kay.

"Put the sword back in the stone," Ector told Arthur. Arthur did this easily. Ector pulled, and Kay pulled, but it stayed in the stone. Then Arthur pulled it out again. At once, Ector and Kay knelt in front of him. "Father, brother, why do you kneel to me?" asked Arthur. "I am not your father, my lord," said Ector softly. "Merlin brought you to me as a little baby." Arthur was very sad. "Please give my son Kay the care of your lands," said Ector. "I promise," said Arthur.

After Christmas, Arthur pulled out the sword in front of the lords. But many lords were angry. "A boy from a small family cannot rule us!" they shouted. They tried again and again for months. Each time, only Arthur could pull out the sword. At Pentecost, all the people came to watch. "Arthur is our king!" the people cried. "We want no more waiting!" The lords knelt and said sorry, and Arthur forgave them. He put the sword on the altar, and soon he became king.

## Level B1

When King Uther Pendragon died, England fell into a dark and dangerous time. Nobody knew of any son who could take his place. Many powerful lords hoped to win the crown.

Merlin, a wise man who knew many secrets, went to see the Archbishop of Canterbury. "Send for all the lords of the land," he said. "Tell them to come to London at Christmas. God will show us the true king by a wonder." The Archbishop trusted Merlin and sent his messengers across the country.

At Christmas, the lords gathered in the greatest church in London. They were praying there before the sun came up. When they came out, they saw something very strange in the churchyard. A great square stone stood there, as white as marble. On top of it sat an anvil of steel with a sword deep inside it. Around the sword ran letters of gold. They said the true king of England was the one who could pull it out.

After the prayers, every lord who wanted the crown tried the sword. Not one of them could make it move even a little. "The man we need is not here yet," said the Archbishop. "But God will make him known." He chose ten good knights and asked them to keep watch over the stone.

The barons planned a great tournament for the first day of the new year. Among the riders was Sir Ector, a good knight with lands near London. With him came his son, Sir Kay, who became a knight only that autumn. Young Arthur rode with them too. He grew up in Ector's home, and Kay was like an older brother to him.

They were riding toward the field when Kay suddenly stopped. "My sword!" he cried. "I left it at our house in town!" He asked Arthur to ride back and fetch it, and Arthur agreed at once.

But when Arthur reached the house, nobody was there. Everyone was out watching the tournament. "I will take the sword from the stone," he said to himself. "My brother will not be without a sword today."

He rode to the churchyard and tied his horse by the gate. The tent of the guards was empty, because they were at the tournament too. Arthur took the sword by its handle and pulled lightly. It slid out of the stone at once. He rode back to Kay and gave it to him.

Kay knew the sword at once and hurried to his father. "Sir, here is the sword of the stone," he said. "So I must be king of this land." Ector looked at the sword in silence. Then he led both young men back to the church. There he made Kay swear on the holy book and tell the truth. "My brother Arthur brought it to me," Kay admitted.

"How did you get this sword?" Ector asked Arthur. Arthur told him the whole story. "Now I understand," said Ector. "You must be the king, because only the true king could pull it out. Put it back, and let me see you do it again." Arthur slid the sword back into the anvil. Ector pulled with all his strength, and so did Kay, but it stayed fast. Then Arthur drew it out as easily as before.

At once Ector knelt down on the ground, and Kay knelt beside him. "My dear father and brother, why are you kneeling to me?" cried Arthur. "No, my lord Arthur," said Ector. "I am not your father, and you are not of my family. Merlin brought you to me as a baby, and I raised you as my own." Arthur was deeply sad to learn that Ector was not his real father. "Will you be a good lord to us when you are king?" asked Ector. "You and your wife raised me, and I owe you more than anyone," said Arthur. Ector asked him to put Kay in charge of his lands, and Arthur agreed.

Twelve days after Christmas, the lords tried again, but only Arthur could move it. Many of them were angry. "Must a boy from a small family rule over us?" they said. So they tried again in early spring, and then at the spring feast. Each time, Arthur pulled out the sword, and each time the lords asked for more time. Meanwhile, the Archbishop placed good knights around Arthur to keep him safe.

At last, at Pentecost, people of every kind gathered to watch. Once more, Arthur drew the sword in front of everyone. "We want Arthur as our king!" the common people shouted. "We can all see that this is God's will!" Then rich and poor knelt and asked him to forgive the long delay. Arthur forgave them all. He laid the sword on the altar, became a knight, and soon afterwards received the crown.

## Level B2

When King Uther Pendragon died, the kingdom was left without a ruler and fell into danger. No one knew of any son who could take the old king's place. Every powerful lord began to dream of the crown, and many gathered men around them.

It was then that Merlin, a man of strange wisdom, went to see the Archbishop of Canterbury. "Send for every lord and every knight in the land," he advised. "Tell them to come to London by Christmas. On that holy day, God will show us by a wonder who should be king." The Archbishop trusted Merlin's advice, and messengers rode out to every corner of the country.

So at Christmas the lords filled the greatest church in London, where they prayed long before daylight. When the first service was over, someone noticed a strange sight in the churchyard. Near the wall, facing the high altar, stood a great square block of stone that looked like marble. In the middle of it sat a steel anvil about a foot high, with a fine sword driven deep into it. Letters of gold ran around the sword. They said that whoever pulled the sword out of the stone and the anvil was the true-born king of all England.

The Archbishop sent everyone back inside to finish their prayers before anyone touched the sword. Afterwards, however, all the lords who wanted to be king tried their strength on it. Not one of them could make the blade move even a hair's width. "The man who will win this sword is not here," said the Archbishop. "But have no doubt that God will make him known." Ten knights of good name were chosen to guard the stone, and it was announced that any man who wished could try.

To keep the lords and the common people together in London, the barons arranged a great tournament for New Year's Day. Among those who rode out to it was Sir Ector, a knight with good lands near London. He brought his son, Sir Kay, who had been made a knight only that autumn. Young Arthur rode with them as well. He had grown up in Ector's house, and he and Kay had always been like brothers.

On the way to the field, Kay suddenly realized that he had left his sword at his father's lodging. He asked Arthur to ride back for it, and Arthur gladly agreed. When Arthur reached the house, however, he found the door shut and no one at home. Ector's wife and all the servants had gone out to watch the tournament.

Arthur was annoyed, but he quickly made up his mind. "I will ride to the churchyard and take the sword that is stuck in the stone," he said to himself. "My brother Kay will not be left without a sword on a day like this." When he arrived, he tied his horse to the stile and walked to the guards' tent. It was empty, because the ten knights had gone to watch the tournament too. Arthur took hold of the sword by its handle and pulled it out lightly, without any effort at all. Then he mounted his horse, rode back to his brother and handed him the sword.

As soon as Kay saw it, he knew it was the sword from the stone. He rode straight to his father. "Sir, look," he said. "Here is the sword of the stone, so I must be king of this land." Ector looked at the sword for a long moment without a word. Then all three of them rode back to the church. Inside, he made Kay place his hand on a holy book and swear to say how he had come by the sword. "My brother Arthur brought it to me," Kay confessed.

Ector turned to Arthur and asked him how he had got it. Arthur explained that he had taken it from the stone because Kay needed a sword, and that no guards had been there. "Now I understand that you must be king of this land," said Ector. "No man could ever draw out this sword unless he was the rightful king. Let me see you put it back as it was and pull it out again." "That is no great task," said Arthur, and he pushed the sword back into the anvil. Ector tried to draw it out and failed, and Kay pulled with all his might without moving it at all. Then Arthur took hold of it and drew it out as easily as before.

At once Ector fell to his knees on the ground, and Kay knelt beside him. "My own dear father and brother, why do you kneel to me?" Arthur cried. "No, my lord Arthur, it is not so," said Ector gently. "I was never your father, and you are not of my family. Merlin brought you to me as a baby to be raised. Now I see that you come from a far higher family than I ever imagined." When Arthur understood that Ector was not his father, he was filled with sorrow.

"Sir, will you be a good and gracious lord to us when you are king?" asked Ector. "I would be wrong to be anything else," replied Arthur. "You and your good wife have raised me as your own, and I owe you more than anyone in the world." Ector asked only one thing: that Kay should be put in charge of all Arthur's lands. Arthur promised that the office would belong to Kay for as long as they both lived.

They went to the Archbishop and told him everything. On the twelfth day after Christmas the barons met again, and once more nobody but Arthur could draw the sword. Many lords were furious. "It would be a great shame for this kingdom to be ruled by a boy of no high birth," they said. So the test was put off until early spring, and then until the spring feast, and each time the same thing happened. Because some lords were bitter, the Archbishop arranged for loyal knights to stay near Arthur day and night.

At last, at Pentecost, people of every rank came to try the sword, and once again only Arthur could draw it. This time the common people would wait no longer. "We want Arthur as our king, and we will not delay any more," they shouted. "We can all see that it is God's will!" Then rich and poor alike knelt before him and begged him to forgive them for keeping him waiting. Arthur forgave them. He took the sword in both hands and laid it on the altar before the Archbishop. That same day he was made a knight. Soon afterwards he was crowned, and he swore to rule with justice all his life.

## Level C1

When Uther Pendragon died, he left behind a kingdom without a ruler, and for a long while the realm stood in great danger. Every lord with an army of his own began to dream of the crown. No son was known to exist. Ambition grew where loyalty had once been, and many great men quietly gathered their followers, each one confident that the prize would fall to him.

It was at this moment that Merlin, whose wisdom seemed to reach further than other men's, came to the Archbishop of Canterbury with a piece of advice. "Send for all the lords of the kingdom and every knight who bears arms," he said. "Command them to come to London by Christmas, for on that holy night God will show by a wonder who ought to rule this land." The Archbishop saw the sense in this, and his messengers soon rode out along every road in Britain.

By Christmas the greatest church in London was full, and the lords knelt in prayer long before the winter sun came up. When they stepped outside after the first service, they found something in the churchyard that had not been there the night before. Against the wall, facing the high altar, stood a great square block of stone, smooth and pale like marble. Set in the middle of it was an anvil of steel, a foot high, and through the anvil, buried almost to the handle, stood a fine sword. Around it, in letters of gold, ran a message: whoever drew the sword from this stone and anvil was the rightful king of all England, born to the crown.

Wonder spread through the crowd, and so did a certain greed. The Archbishop, however, would allow no one near the stone until the prayers inside were finished, and only then did he let the lords take their turn. One after another, the proudest men in the kingdom set their hands on the handle and stepped back red-faced, for the blade would not move so much as a hair. "The man who is meant to win this sword is not among us," the Archbishop declared calmly, "but do not doubt that God will make him known in time." He appointed ten knights of honest reputation to guard the stone, and it was proclaimed through the city that any man who wished might come and try his strength.

Fearing that the lords would drift home and the country slip back into disorder, the barons arranged a great tournament for New Year's Day. They hoped it would keep everyone in London until the riddle was solved. Among the knights who rode out to it was Sir Ector, a man of good lands and quiet worth who lived not far from the city. With him rode his son, Sir Kay, who had been made a knight only at the start of that winter and was eager to prove himself. Behind them came young Arthur, who had been brought up in Ector's household from his earliest days and had always looked on Kay as his elder brother.

They were well on their way to the field when Kay put his hand to his side and found nothing there. In his excitement he had left his sword at his father's lodging in the town. He begged Arthur to ride back and fetch it, and Arthur, always willing, turned his horse at once. The house, when he reached it, was shut up and silent; the lady of the house and every last servant had gone out to watch the jousting.

Arthur sat on his horse in the empty street, more vexed than he liked to admit, and then a thought came to him that was as simple as it was bold. "I will ride to the churchyard and take the sword that sits in the stone," he said to himself. "My brother shall not be without a sword on this of all days." When he arrived, he tied his horse to the stile and went over to the guards' tent, only to find it empty. The ten knights had gone off to the tournament as well. He grasped the sword by its handle, gave a light, quick pull, and drew it from the stone as easily as if it had been resting in a scabbard. Without a second glance at what he had done, he mounted, rode back to Kay and put the sword into his hands.

Kay knew it the instant he saw it. Without a word to Arthur he galloped to his father, holding the blade high. "Sir," he said, "here is the sword of the stone, which means that I must be king of this land." Ector studied the sword for a long time, saying nothing at all. Then he turned back towards the church, and the three of them rode there together and went inside. Before the altar, Ector made his son lay a hand upon a holy book and swear to tell him truthfully how he had come by the sword. "My brother Arthur brought it to me," he admitted.

Ector then asked Arthur how he had got it, and Arthur, puzzled by the fuss, explained that he had needed a sword for Kay and found no guards there. "Now I understand," said Ector slowly, "that you must be king of this land, for no man could ever draw out this sword except the one who is rightfully king. Let me see whether you can put it back where it was and pull it out once more." "There is nothing difficult in that," said Arthur, and he slid the blade back into the anvil. Ector seized the handle and strained until his arms shook, yet it would not stir; Kay, ordered to try in his turn, pulled with all his might and did no better. Arthur then stepped forward, and the sword came out in his hand as lightly as before.

Ector sank to his knees on the stone floor, and Kay, after a moment's hesitation, knelt beside him. "My own dear father and brother," Arthur cried, "why are you kneeling to me?" "No, my lord Arthur, it is not so," Ector answered gently. "I was never your father, nor are you any kin of mine, and I can see now that your birth is far higher than I ever guessed." He told him how, long ago, Merlin had brought him as a tiny child and given him into Ector's care to be raised. Hearing this, Arthur was overcome with grief, for it is a hard thing to win a kingdom and lose a father in the same breath.

"Sir," said Ector, "will you be my good and gracious lord when you are king?" "I would deserve blame if I were anything else," Arthur replied. "There is no one alive to whom I owe more than to you and to my good lady, your wife, who cared for me as tenderly as for her own son." Ector asked for a single favor in return: that Kay should be the steward of all Arthur's lands. Arthur gave his word that the office would belong to Kay and to no other man for as long as the two of them lived.

They went straight to the Archbishop and told him what had happened. On the twelfth day after Christmas the barons came together once more, and once more the sword refused every hand but Arthur's. The lords did not take it well. They declared that it would be a disgrace for the whole realm to be governed by a boy of no high birth, and they insisted on waiting. The trial was put off until early spring, and then to the spring feast, and each time the outcome was exactly the same. Meanwhile, because certain lords had grown bitter, the Archbishop, on Merlin's advice, saw to it that trusted knights stayed close to Arthur by day and by night.

At Pentecost, when men of every rank came to try the sword, it was once again Arthur alone who drew it, this time before the eyes of lords and common people together. It was the common people who would bear the waiting no longer. "We will have Arthur for our king, and we will not put it off another day," they cried, "for any fool can see that this is God's will!" Rich and poor alike then fell to their knees and begged his pardon for having kept him so long from his right. Arthur forgave them freely. Taking the sword in both hands, he laid it upon the altar where the Archbishop stood, and there he was made a knight by the best man present. Not long afterwards he was crowned, swearing before lords and commons alike to be a true king and to rule with justice for the rest of his days.
