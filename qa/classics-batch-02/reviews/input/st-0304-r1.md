# The Prince and the Pauper

Summary: A poor boy and a young prince change clothes, and each one starts to live the other's life.

## Level A1

Tom Canty was a poor boy in London. He lived in Offal Court. His father, John Canty, was hard and angry. But Tom had a big dream. He wanted to see a real prince.

One morning, Tom walked to Westminster. At the palace gate, he saw Prince Edward. A guard pushed Tom away. "Stop! Let the boy in!" cried the prince.

The prince took Tom to his room. He gave Tom good food. "I want fine clothes like yours," said Tom. "Then take my clothes," said the prince. The two boys changed clothes. They stood in front of a big mirror. They looked the same!

Then the prince saw Tom's red hand. The guard's push hurt it. First, the prince hid the Great Seal. Then he ran out to the gate. "I am the prince!" said Edward. The guard laughed at the boy in rags. He pushed him out into the street.

Nobody in the city believed Edward. Then John Canty found him. "You are my son Tom!" said John Canty. The boy ran away from him. Then a brave soldier, Miles Hendon, helped him. Miles did not believe the boy's story. But he kept the boy safe.

At the palace, Tom was sad. "I am not the prince," said Tom. "The prince is ill," said the lords. Then old King Henry died. Now Tom was the new king! He was a kind and fair king.

Later, Tom sat in a great church. A lord lifted the crown. Suddenly a boy in rags ran in. "Stop! I am the king!" cried the boy. "Yes! He is the king!" said Tom.

"Where is the Great Seal?" asked the lords. Tom helped Edward remember. It was in an old suit of armor. Tom used it to crack nuts!

Edward got his crown. He was a good king to the poor. Tom and his mother had a good home. Miles Hendon became a great lord.

## Level A2

Tom Canty and Prince Edward had the same birthday. But their lives were very different. Edward lived in a palace, and everyone loved him. Tom lived in Offal Court, a poor and dirty street in London. His father, John Canty, was a rough and angry man. He sent Tom out to beg for money every day. A kind old priest taught Tom to read. Tom loved his stories about kings. Tom wanted to see a real prince just once.

One morning, Tom walked all the way to Westminster. Through the palace gate, he saw Prince Edward in fine clothes. A guard pulled him back and pushed him away. "How dare you! Open the gate!" cried the prince. The prince took Tom into his own rooms. He gave him good food and asked about his life.

"I want to wear clothes like yours, just once," said Tom. "Then let us change clothes," said the prince. The boys changed clothes and stood in front of a tall mirror. They had the same face, the same eyes and the same hair!

Then Edward saw a red mark on Tom's hand. "The guard did this!" said Edward angrily. First, he quickly hid the Great Seal of England. Then he ran out to the gate in Tom's rags. The guard laughed and pushed the boy out into the street.

In the city, Edward told everyone, "I am the prince!" But people only laughed at the boy in rags. Then John Canty found him. "You are my son, and you must come home!" shouted John Canty. Edward got away from him in a big crowd. Later, an angry crowd pushed him around. Then a tall soldier stepped forward. His name was Miles Hendon. "Leave the boy alone!" said Miles, with his hand on his sword. The crowd went away.

Miles took Edward to a small room at an inn. He did not believe the boy's story. "Poor child, his mind is ill," thought Miles. But he was kind to him and kept him safe. Edward made Miles a knight, and Miles smiled and played along.

At the palace, Tom told everyone the truth. "I am Tom Canty from Offal Court," he said. "The prince is ill," said the lords sadly. Soon old King Henry died, and Tom became the new king! He was kind and fair, and he helped poor people.

On the big day, everyone went to the great church at Westminster. A lord lifted the crown above Tom's head. Suddenly a boy in rags ran in. "Stop! I am the true king!" cried the boy. Tom knew him at once. "He is right, my lords!" said Tom.

"Where is the Great Seal?" asked one lord. Edward thought hard, but he could not remember. Tom helped him with some questions. The seal was inside an old suit of armor. Tom used it to crack nuts! So the true king got his crown. Edward was a good king, and he never forgot the poor. Tom lived well with his mother all his life. Miles Hendon became a great lord.

## Level B1

On the same autumn day in London, two baby boys came into the world. One of them was Tom Canty, the son of a poor family in Offal Court. The other was Edward, the son of King Henry and the prince of all England.

Tom grew up in a dark, crowded house. His father, John Canty, was a hard and angry man who sent him out to beg. When Tom came home with nothing, his father shouted at him and treated him roughly. His mother was gentle, and she often saved her own bread for him. A kind old priest taught Tom to read and told him about kings. More than anything, he wanted to see a real prince.

One morning Tom walked through the city until he reached the palace at Westminster. Behind the tall gate stood Prince Edward in beautiful clothes. At once a guard pushed Tom back into the crowd. "How dare you treat a poor boy like that? Open the gate and let him in!" shouted the prince.

Edward led Tom to his own rooms and ordered a fine meal for him. While Tom was eating, the prince asked him many questions about his life. Tom told him about the games in Offal Court. "I would love to play in the mud just once," said the prince with a sigh. "And I would love to wear clothes like yours just once," said Tom. "Then let us change clothes right now!" said Edward.

Soon the two boys stood side by side before a tall mirror. They had the same face, the same eyes and the same hair. "Nobody could tell us apart," said the prince in surprise. Then he noticed a red mark on Tom's hand from the guard. "The guard hurt you! Wait here," said Edward. Before he left, he quickly put away an important object, the Great Seal of England. Then he ran out to the gate in Tom's old rags.

The guard saw only a dirty beggar boy. Edward said he was the prince, but the guard laughed and pushed him out. Edward walked through London for hours, cold and hungry, but nobody believed him. At night John Canty found him and took him by the arm. "You are coming home with me, Tom!" said John Canty roughly. Later that night the boy escaped from him in a large, noisy crowd.

The next evening an angry crowd began to push him around. Then a tall soldier with a sword stepped between the boy and the people. His name was Miles Hendon, and he was just back from many years at war. "Leave him alone," said Miles calmly. Nobody wanted to fight him, so the crowd went away.

Miles took Edward to a small room at an inn near the river. He did not believe the boy's story, but he felt sorry for him. "The poor child has lost his mind, but he is brave," thought Miles. To thank him, Edward made him a knight and gave him a special right. From now on, Miles could sit down in front of the king. For weeks, they faced many dangers together, and Miles never left the boy.

Meanwhile, Tom was living a strange new life at the palace. At first, he told everyone the truth, but nobody listened. "The prince is ill and has forgotten who he is," the lords whispered. They taught him how to eat and speak like a prince. When the lords asked him for the Great Seal, Tom had no idea what it was. Then old King Henry died, and Tom became the king of England. He was frightened, but he listened to people and judged them fairly. He freed innocent people from prison, and the lords began to admire him.

At last came the day of the crowning in the great church at Westminster. A lord was holding the crown above Tom's head when a boy in rags ran in. "Stop! I am the king of England!" cried the boy. Tom knew him at once and fell to his knees. "He is the true king, my lords!" said Tom.

The lords did not know which boy to believe. "Only the true king knows where to find the Great Seal," said one lord. A lord searched Edward's room, but the seal was not there. Then Tom asked Edward questions about that morning, and suddenly Edward remembered. The seal was inside the arm of an old suit of armor. The lords looked there and found it. "I used it to crack nuts!" said Tom. So Edward got his crown at last. Later Miles came to the palace and sat down in front of the king. Edward ruled kindly, because he never forgot his days among the poor. Tom lived happily with his mother, and Miles became a great lord.

## Level B2

On an autumn day in London, two boys were born within hours of each other. One was Edward, the son of King Henry, and the whole of England celebrated his arrival with bells and bonfires. The other was Tom Canty, the son of a poor family in Offal Court, and nobody celebrated at all.

Tom's father, John Canty, was a rough, bad-tempered man who sent his son out to beg in the streets every day. If Tom came home without money, his father shouted at him and sent him to bed without supper. His mother loved him, and late at night she often slipped him a piece of bread she had saved. An old priest in the neighborhood taught him to read and filled his head with stories of kings and courts. Soon Tom was playing at being a prince with the other children, who treated him as a little ruler. But his greatest wish was to see a real prince, just once.

One morning his wandering feet carried him all the way to the royal palace at Westminster. Through the bars of the great gate he saw a boy his own age, dressed in silk and jewels. It was Prince Edward. He pressed his face to the bars, but a guard grabbed him and threw him roughly back into the crowd. "How dare you treat a poor boy like that? Open the gates at once!" cried the prince.

Edward took Tom up to his own rooms and ordered a splendid meal for him. While Tom ate, he described the puppet shows, the dancing and the river swimming of Offal Court. "I would give my crown to play in the mud like that just once," said Edward. "And I would love to wear fine clothes like yours just once," Tom replied shyly. The prince laughed and suggested that they exchange clothes immediately.

When they stood side by side before a tall mirror, both boys stared in amazement. They had the same face, the same eyes, the same hair and even the same voice. "If someone saw us now, nobody could tell which of us was the prince," said Edward. Then he noticed a bruise on Tom's hand and realized that the guard had caused it. Angry, he quickly put away an important object from the table, the Great Seal of England, and ran out of the room. He was still wearing Tom's rags.

At the gate, the guard saw nothing but a ragged beggar boy who claimed to be the prince. He laughed, pushed the boy out into the road and shut the gate behind him. For hours Edward wandered through London, hungry and cold. That night John Canty seized him, sure that he had found his own son Tom. "You'll come home and stop this nonsense," said John Canty, pulling the boy along. In a noisy crowd near the river, however, the boy managed to slip away.

The next evening Edward tried to enter a great hall where the city was holding a feast for the prince. The crowd turned against him. At that moment a tall soldier in worn clothes stepped forward with his hand on his sword. His name was Miles Hendon, and he had just returned from years of fighting abroad. "Prince or not, this boy is brave, and I will protect him," said Miles. The crowd backed away, and just then a messenger arrived with serious news: King Henry was dead. Edward understood that he was now the king of England, and he wept for his father.

Miles took the boy to his small room at an inn on London Bridge. He believed that the child's mind was disturbed, but he admired his courage and decided to look after him. When Edward ordered him to serve dinner, Miles played along with a smile. To reward him, Edward made him a knight and granted him a rare right: to sit in the king's presence.

The following weeks were hard. John Canty tricked Edward away from Miles, and the boy spent days among thieves and beggars on the country roads. Everywhere he saw how hard the laws were on poor people, and he promised himself he would change them. Miles found him again, and together they traveled to Miles's old home in the country. There, however, Miles's younger brother pretended not to know him, and the two friends ended up in prison. Even so, Miles took a punishment meant for the boy, and Edward never forgot it. At last, they were set free and left for London together.

Meanwhile, Tom was learning to live in the palace. At first, he told everyone the truth, but the lords decided that the prince was ill. Patiently, they taught him how to dress, eat and speak like royalty. When they asked him for the Great Seal, he had no idea what they meant. After King Henry's death, the frightened boy found himself king of England.

To everyone's surprise, Tom proved a good judge. He listened carefully to every case and freed several people who had been unfairly accused. He also softened some of the cruel laws, and the lords began to admire the young king's mercy. Yet on the day of the great procession through London, he saw his mother in the crowd. She recognized him at once, but he turned away and said, "I do not know you, woman." He felt deeply ashamed for the rest of the day.

The coronation took place in the great church at Westminster. Just as the crown was about to be placed on Tom's head, a boy in rags ran forward. "Stop! I am the true king of England!" cried the boy. Tom recognized him instantly and knelt before him. "He is the king, my lords," Tom said joyfully. The lords did not know which boy to believe, so they asked a question only the real king could answer. Where was the Great Seal? At first Edward sent a lord to search his cabinet, but the seal was not there. Then Tom asked him to think back to that morning. Suddenly Edward remembered putting the seal inside the arm of an old suit of armor, and there it was. "So that is what it was! I used it to crack nuts," said Tom.

Edward was crowned at last. Soon after, Miles came to the palace, and to the guards' astonishment he calmly sat down in front of the king. Edward confirmed that this was his right, and he made Miles an earl. Tom became the King's Ward, and he lived in honor with his mother for the rest of his life. As for Edward, he ruled kindly, because he had seen with his own eyes how the poor lived.

## Level C1

On a single autumn day in crowded London, two boys came into the world within a few hours of each other. One was Edward, the long-awaited son of King Henry, and the whole of England greeted his birth with bells and bonfires. The other was Tom Canty, born to a poor family in Offal Court, and nobody except his tired mother paid his arrival the slightest attention. Though they grew up only a few miles apart, the prince and the pauper might as well have lived on different stars.

Tom's father, John Canty, was a rough and bad-tempered man who made his living by begging and small tricks, and he expected his son to do the same. On the evenings when Tom came home with empty hands, his father shouted at him and sent him to bed without supper. His mother, gentle and worn out, would often creep to his corner late at night with a crust of bread she had saved from her own share. These small kindnesses, together with the lessons of an old priest who lived nearby, were the only bright things in Tom's childhood.

The priest taught him to read a little Latin as well as English, and filled his head with stories of kings, princes and splendid courts. Tom read every old book he could find, and his manners grew so refined that the other children began to treat him as a kind of ruler. They brought him their quarrels to settle, and he held a pretend court in the street, handing out titles and judgments with great seriousness. Yet behind all his games lay one burning wish: to see a real prince, just once, with his own eyes.

One morning his wandering feet carried him all the way to the royal palace at Westminster. Through the bars of the great gate he caught sight of a slim boy of his own age, dressed in silk and sparkling jewels. It was Prince Edward, and for a moment Tom forgot his hunger, his rags and everything else in the world. Without thinking, he pressed his face against the bars, but a guard seized him and flung him back into the crowd of staring onlookers. "How dare you treat a poor boy like that? Open the gates at once, and let him in!" the prince cried, his face red with anger.

Edward led his astonished guest up to his private rooms and ordered a splendid meal, then sent the servants away so that Tom would not feel shy. While Tom ate, he described the puppet shows, the dancing and the summer afternoons spent splashing in the river. "I would give up my crown to roll in the mud like that, just once, with nobody to scold me," Edward sighed. "And I would give anything to wear clothes like yours, just once," Tom answered. Delighted, the prince proposed that they exchange clothes then and there.

When the two boys stood side by side in front of a tall mirror, they stared in silent amazement. The same face, the same eyes and the same hair looked back at them; even their voices were alike, and only their clothes told them apart. "Were we to walk out like this, not one person in the palace could say which of us is the prince," Edward said slowly. It was then that he noticed a bruise on Tom's hand, and when he learned that the guard had caused it, his anger returned. Pausing only to put away an object of great importance that lay on the table, the Great Seal of England, he hurried out of the room, still dressed in Tom's rags.

At the gate, the guard saw nothing but a dirty beggar who claimed to be the heir to the throne. With a laugh, he pushed the boy out into the road. For the rest of the day Edward wandered through London, cold, hungry and bewildered, discovering how quickly people laugh at a boy in rags. When night fell, a heavy hand landed on his shoulder, for John Canty had found him and was certain he had caught his own runaway son. "A prince, are you? We'll soon cure you of that," said John Canty, pulling the boy toward Offal Court. Later that night, in a noisy crowd near the river, the boy slipped from his grasp.

The next evening the city held a great feast for the prince, and Edward tried to push his way into the hall to claim his place. The crowd turned on him at once, and he might have come to real trouble had a tall stranger not stepped in front of him with his hand on his sword. The stranger was Miles Hendon, a soldier just home from years of fighting abroad, his clothes faded but his manner proud. "Prince or no prince, this lad has courage, and nobody touches him while I stand here," Miles declared, and the crowd drew back. At that very moment a messenger rode up with solemn news: King Henry was dead. Edward understood that his father was gone and that he himself was now king, and for a while he could not stop weeping.

Miles took the boy to his small room at an inn on London Bridge, convinced that the poor child had lost his wits but charmed by his dignity. When Edward ordered him to stand and serve the meal, Miles obeyed with a hidden smile, deciding that it cost nothing to humor such a brave little dreamer. Grateful for his rescue, the young king made him a knight and granted him, at his own request, a rare privilege: the right to sit in the king's presence.

The weeks that followed were the hardest of Edward's life. John Canty lured him away from Miles with a false message, and the boy was forced to wander the country roads with a band of thieves and beggars. Wherever he went, he saw how harshly the laws treated the poor, and he silently promised that, once he had his throne back, he would change them. Miles searched until he found him, and together they traveled to Miles's family home in the country, only to discover that his younger brother refused to recognize him. The two companions ended up in prison, where Miles quietly accepted a punishment meant for the boy, a sacrifice Edward never forgot. At last, they were released and headed for London together.

Meanwhile, in the palace, Tom was living a dream that had quickly turned into a puzzle. At first, he insisted to anyone who would listen that he was only Tom Canty of Offal Court, but the lords concluded that the prince's mind was ill. Patiently, they taught him how to dress, dine and receive visitors, and he soon stopped making mistakes. When the lords asked him where the Great Seal was, however, he had no idea what they meant, and the seal could not be found anywhere. Then the old king died, and the frightened boy from Offal Court found himself king of England.

To everyone's surprise, the new king proved to be a wise and merciful judge. He listened carefully to every case brought before him, freed several people who had been unjustly accused, and softened some of the harshest laws in the land. Yet his hardest test came on the day of the royal procession through London, when he saw his own mother pushing through the crowd toward him. She knew him at once, but he turned his face away and said, "I do not know you, woman," and the words weighed on his heart for the rest of the day.

The coronation took place in the great church at Westminster. Just as the crown was about to be lowered onto Tom's head, a boy in rags ran down the aisle and cried out, "Stop! I am the king!" Tom recognized him instantly and knelt before him, declaring, "He speaks the truth, my lords." Not knowing which boy to believe, the lords put a single question that only the real prince could answer: where was the Great Seal of England? Edward sent a lord to search a cabinet in his room, but the seal was not there. It was Tom who saved the moment, gently asking Edward to think back to that morning, until he remembered putting the seal inside the arm of an old suit of armor. There it was found, exactly where he said. "So that is what it was! I have been using it to crack nuts," said Tom.

Edward was crowned at last, and when Miles Hendon arrived at the palace soon after, he calmly sat down before the king, while the guards stared in shock. Edward confirmed that this was his right, gave Miles back his family home and made him an earl. Tom was named the King's Ward and given a place of honor, and he lived comfortably with his mother for the rest of his days. As for Edward, his reign was short, but it was a merciful one, for he never forgot the hunger and injustice he had seen with his own eyes.
