# The Little Mermaid

Summary: A young mermaid gives up her voice for a chance to live among humans on land.

## Level A1

The sea king had six daughters. They were mermaids with long fish tails. The youngest was quiet and very pretty. She loved stories about the land. At fifteen, she swam up to the top. She saw a big ship with lights. A young prince stood on it.

Then a storm came. The ship broke, and the prince fell. The little mermaid swam to him. She took him to a beach. A girl from a white house found him. He smiled at the girl. He did not see the mermaid.

"Can I live on land?" she asked. "People have a soul," said her grandmother. "Mermaids become sea foam in the end." "A man must love you and marry you." "Then you can have a soul too."

She went to the sea witch. "Give me your voice," said the witch. "Then you can have legs." "But every step hurts like a knife." "He must marry you, or you become foam." The mermaid gave her voice.

Now she had legs, but no voice. Every step hurt, but she smiled. She danced for the prince. He liked her very much. But he loved the girl from the beach.

Then he met a princess. She was the girl from the beach! "You saved me!" he said. They married on a big ship. The little mermaid danced, but her heart hurt.

At night, her sisters came. Their long hair was gone. "We paid the witch with our hair." "Here is her knife." "Use it on the prince before morning." "Then you can come home." She looked at the sleeping prince. But she loved him too much. She threw the knife into the waves. Then she jumped into the sea.

Her body turned into foam. But she did not die. She went up into the warm air. "Come with us," said kind voices. They were the daughters of the air. She smiled at the prince one last time. Then she flew up with them.

## Level A2

Deep in the ocean stood the sea king's palace. He lived there with his mother and six daughters. The girls were mermaids with fish tails, not feet. The youngest was the most beautiful and the quietest. Her grandmother told her about the world above. "At fifteen, you can swim up and see it," she said.

At last, the youngest turned fifteen. She swam up to the top at sunset. She saw a large ship full of music and lights. There was a party for a young prince. It was his sixteenth birthday. The little mermaid could not stop looking at him.

Late that night, a terrible storm began. The ship broke apart, and the prince fell into the sea. All night, she held his head above the waves. In the morning, she carried him to a quiet beach. Then she hid behind some rocks. A girl from a white building found him there. The prince woke up and smiled at the girl. He never knew the true story.

"Can people live forever?" the little mermaid asked her grandmother. "No, but they have a soul, and it never dies." "We live for three hundred years and then become foam." "A man must love you and marry you." "Then part of his soul goes into you."

So the little mermaid swam to the sea witch. "I can give you legs," said the witch. "But every step can feel like a sharp knife." "The prince must marry you, or your heart breaks." "Then you become foam on the sea." "And I want your beautiful voice." The little mermaid said yes and drank the witch's drink.

At sunrise, the prince found her on his steps. She had two legs now, but she could not speak. Every step hurt, but she walked lightly, like a bird. She danced, and everyone loved it. He liked her very much, like a little sister. "You look like the girl from the beach," he told her. "I love only her."

Then the prince visited a nearby king. The king's daughter was the girl from the beach! "You saved me!" cried the prince, very happy. They married on a large ship. She danced at the party, but her heart hurt.

That night, her sisters rose out of the water. Their long hair was gone. "We gave our hair to the witch for this knife," they said. "Use it on the prince before the sun comes up." "Then you can be a mermaid again." The little mermaid looked at the sleeping prince. She loved him too much for that. She threw the knife away and jumped into the sea.

Her body turned into foam, but she did not die. Soft voices sang around her, and she rose into the air. "We are the daughters of the air," they said. "We do good things, and one day we get a soul." She smiled down at the prince for the last time. Then she flew up with her new sisters.

## Level B1

Far out in the ocean, the water is clear and very deep. At the bottom stood the palace of the sea king. He lived there with his mother and six daughters, who were all mermaids. The youngest was the quietest, and she loved her grandmother's stories. They were about ships, towns and birds that sang in the trees. "At fifteen, you can swim up and see it all," she promised.

At last, the youngest turned fifteen and rose to the top of the sea. A large ship was resting on the water, and music was playing on board. The sailors were celebrating the birthday of a young prince with dark, friendly eyes. The little mermaid could not stop looking at him.

Late that night, a terrible storm hit the ship and broke it apart. The prince fell into the sea and disappeared under the dark water. The little mermaid swam to him and held his head above the waves. By morning, she reached a beach near a large white building. She laid him on the warm sand and hid behind some rocks. Soon a young girl came out of the building and found him. The prince woke and smiled at the girl, but he never saw the mermaid.

After that, the little mermaid often swam near the prince's palace at night. She asked her grandmother whether humans could live forever. "No, but they have a soul, which lives on after death," she answered. "We live for three hundred years and then turn into foam." "A man must love you more than his parents and marry you," she said. "Then you will get a soul too, but it will never happen."

The little mermaid went to the sea witch, although she feared her. "My drink will turn your tail into legs," said the witch. "But every step will feel like walking on sharp knives." "And when the prince marries another girl, your heart will break." "The next morning, you will become foam on the sea." "As payment, I want your voice, the most beautiful voice in the sea." The little mermaid thought of the prince and agreed.

Before sunrise, she drank the magic drink on the steps of the prince's palace. When she woke up, the prince was standing in front of her. Her tail was gone, and in its place were two pretty legs. She could not speak, but the prince took her into his palace. Every step hurt her badly, yet she moved as lightly as a bubble. When she danced, nobody could take their eyes off her.

The prince liked her very much, but he loved her like a sweet child. "You remind me of the girl who saved my life," he told her. "She lives in a holy house, and I will never see her again." The little mermaid could not tell him the truth. At night, she cooled her burning feet in the sea. Sometimes her sisters came and sang to her.

Soon the prince sailed to meet the daughter of a nearby king. When he saw the princess, he cried out with joy. She was the girl from the white building by the sea. "You are the one who saved me!" he said. The little mermaid kissed his hand, but she felt deep pain inside.

The wedding took place on a large ship, with music and golden lamps. That night, she danced better than ever, although each step felt like a knife. She knew that this was her last night. Late at night, her sisters rose out of the waves without their long hair. "The witch took our hair and gave us this knife," they said. "Use it on the prince before the sun comes up, and you can come home."

She went quietly to the prince's tent and looked at his sleeping face. In his dream, he was saying his bride's name. Her hand shook, and then she threw the knife far into the waves. She looked at the prince one last time and jumped into the sea.

Her body began to turn into foam, but she did not feel cold. Instead, she felt herself rising into the warm air among hundreds of light, clear beings. "We are the daughters of the air," they told her. "We can earn a soul with good deeds, and now you can join us." Down on the ship, the prince and his bride were looking sadly at the foam. Unseen, she smiled at the prince and rose into the pink clouds.

## Level B2

Far out at sea, the water is as blue as a summer flower and as clear as glass. At the bottom, among tall plants swaying in the current, stands the palace of the sea king. The king's wife had died many years before, so his old mother ran the household. She loved her six granddaughters dearly, lovely mermaids with tails instead of feet. The youngest was a strange, quiet child whose only treasure was a stone statue of a handsome boy.

Nothing pleased her more than her grandmother's stories about the human world above. "When you turn fifteen, you will be allowed to rise to the surface," the old woman promised. The sisters were born a year apart, so the youngest had to wait the longest of all. Each sister returned from her first visit with a hundred things to describe. One spoke of a great city whose lights shone like stars, and another of a sunset in clouds of gold. The youngest listened to every word and longed for her own turn.

At last, she turned fifteen and rose as lightly as a bubble to the surface at sunset. Close by lay a large ship, with music and singing coming from its deck. When darkness fell, hundreds of colored lamps were lit to celebrate a young prince's sixteenth birthday. He was the most handsome person she had ever seen.

Late in the night, the sea grew restless, and lightning flashed through the black clouds. Soon the waves rose like mountains, the mast broke in two, and the prince was thrown into the sea. At first, she was delighted, thinking he would now come down to her home. Then she remembered that humans cannot live in water, and she dived among the floating wood to find him. She held his head above the waves through the long night, and by morning the storm was over. She carried him to a shore where a large white building stood among lemon trees. After laying him on the warm sand, she hid behind some rocks and covered her hair with foam. Before long, a young girl came out of the building and found the prince. He woke and smiled at the people around him, but he had no idea who had really saved him.

From then on, the little mermaid grew quieter, and her sisters helped her find the prince's palace. Many nights she floated near his balcony, watching him in secret. "Do humans live forever?" she asked her grandmother one day. "No, and their lives are much shorter than ours," the old woman replied. "We live for three hundred years, but when we die, we simply become foam on the water." "Humans, however, have a soul that lives forever and rises up to the shining stars." "If a man loved you more than his father and mother and married you, his soul would flow into you." "But that can never happen, because people on land think a fish's tail is ugly."

That evening, the little mermaid slipped away to visit the sea witch, whom she had always feared. "I know exactly what you want," said the witch, "and it is very foolish of you." "I will make you a drink, and your tail will divide into two legs." "It will hurt like a sword cutting through you, and every step will feel like walking on knives." "If the prince marries another woman, your heart will break the next morning, and you will turn into foam." "In return, I want the most precious thing you own: your voice." The little mermaid went pale, but she agreed, and the witch took her voice away.

Before sunrise, she reached the prince's palace and drank the drink on the broad stone steps. The pain was so terrible that she fainted, and when she woke, the prince was standing over her. She could not speak a single word. He led her inside, and although each step was agony, she moved more gracefully than anyone there. When she danced, everyone was enchanted, and the prince declared that she must always stay with him. She was allowed to sleep on a velvet cushion outside his door.

She rode beside him through the forests and climbed high mountains at his side, though her feet bled. At night, she cooled her burning feet in the sea while her sisters sang sadly to her. The prince grew fonder of her every day, but he loved her as one loves a good child. "You are like the girl who found me on the beach after the shipwreck," he told her. "She is the only one I could ever love, but she belongs to a holy house." The little mermaid could not tell him the truth, and she sighed without a sound.

Before long, the prince sailed to a neighboring kingdom to meet the king's daughter. When the princess came to greet him, he cried out in joy. She was the girl from the white building by the sea. "It was you who saved me!" he said, and he asked the little mermaid to share his happiness. She kissed his hand and felt as if her heart were already breaking.

The wedding was held that very day, and in the evening the couple boarded a splendid ship. Lamps glowed in every color, and the little mermaid danced more beautifully than ever. This was the last night she would see the prince, for whom she had given up everything. After midnight, the prince and his bride went to their royal tent, and the ship grew silent.

Suddenly, her pale sisters rose out of the waves, and their long hair was gone. "We gave our hair to the witch, and she gave us this knife," they said. "Before the sun rises, you must use it against the prince, and then your legs will become a tail again." "You can come home and live out your three hundred years with us."

She drew back the curtain of the tent and saw the prince asleep beside his bride. In his dreams he spoke his bride's name, for she alone was in his thoughts. The knife trembled in her hand, and then she threw it far out into the waves. She looked at the prince once more and threw herself from the ship into the sea.

She felt her body dissolving into foam, but the sun rose warm and gentle, and she did not die. Instead, she rose among hundreds of clear, beautiful beings. "We are the daughters of the air," they told her. "Like mermaids, we have no soul, but we can earn one by doing good deeds for three hundred years." On the ship, the prince and his bride were searching for her, gazing sadly at the foam. Unseen, she kissed the bride's forehead, smiled at the prince, and rose with the others toward a pink cloud.

## Level C1

Far out at sea, the water is as blue as a cornflower and as clear as glass, yet deeper than any anchor rope can measure. Down there, where strange trees sway at the faintest stir of the current, stands the palace of the sea king, its walls of coral and its roof of shells. The king had long been a widower, and his household was run by his aged mother, a proud and clever woman who wore twelve oysters on her tail. Other ladies of rank were permitted only six.

The youngest of her six granddaughters was the loveliest, with eyes as blue as the deepest water and, like her sisters, a fish's tail instead of feet. She was a strange, quiet child, and while the others filled their gardens with odd treasures from wrecked ships, she planted only flowers as red as the sun. Her one other treasure was a marble statue of a handsome boy, which had sunk to the sea floor after some long-forgotten shipwreck. Nothing gave her greater joy than tales of the world above, where flowers had a scent and birds, which her grandmother called fish, sang in the trees.

"When you reach your fifteenth year," the old woman promised, "you may rise to the surface and watch the great ships sail by." Born a year apart, the sisters went up one by one, leaving the youngest to wait five long years. The eldest came back speaking of a great city whose lights glittered like countless stars, and of church bells whose sound drifted far across the water.

When her own birthday came, the oysters her grandmother fastened to her tail pinched painfully, but beauty, the old lady said, always has its price. Then she rose as lightly as a bubble, and the sun had only just set when she lifted her head above the waves. A great ship lay in the calm water, music drifting from its deck, and as darkness gathered, hundreds of colored lanterns flared into life. The sailors were celebrating the sixteenth birthday of a young prince with large dark eyes, and she could look at nothing else.

Long after midnight, the sea grew restless and lightning tore the clouds apart. The ship plunged among waves as high as mountains, until its great mast snapped like a reed and the vessel rolled onto its side. For one foolish moment she rejoiced, before remembering that humans cannot breathe in water and that he would reach her father's palace only in death. She dived through the wreckage until she found him, his strength almost gone, and held his head above the waves while the current carried them both.

By morning the storm had passed, though the prince's eyes stayed shut, and she thought he resembled the marble boy in her garden. She laid him on the sand near a white building, perhaps a convent, then withdrew behind some rocks, hiding her face in the foam. Presently a bell rang, young girls came out into the garden, and one of them found the stranger and ran to fetch help. The prince came back to life and smiled at everyone around him, but he had no idea who had truly saved him.

At last, she confided in one of her sisters, and before long a mermaid who knew every coast led them to his palace. From then on she spent her nights beneath its balcony, listening gladly as the fishermen praised him.

"Do humans live forever?" she asked her grandmother, and learned that their lives were even shorter than a mermaid's three hundred years. "Yet when our time ends, we are only foam upon the water, while human beings have a soul that rises through the clear air to the stars," the old woman said. "I would gladly trade every one of my centuries for a single human day," the little mermaid said sadly. There was one way, her grandmother admitted: a man would have to love her more than his own parents and pledge himself to her before a priest. "Then part of his soul would flow into you, but it can never happen, for people on land think a fish's tail is hideous."

That night, while the sea king's court held a great ball, the little mermaid slipped away to the sea witch, whom she had always dreaded. The way led past roaring whirlpools and through a forest of grasping growths that seized anything drifting near. "I know what you want," the witch said with a dreadful laugh, "and you shall have it, for it will bring you nothing but sorrow." She promised to brew a drink that would split the tail in two, and warned that the change would feel like a sword cutting through the girl's body. Afterward, she would glide more gracefully than any dancer on earth, yet every step would feel as though she were treading on sharp knives. "And should the prince marry another, your heart will break on the following morning, and you will become nothing but foam upon the waves." Her price was the girl's voice, the sweetest in the whole ocean, and although the little mermaid turned pale, she bowed her head and paid it.

The sun had not yet risen when she climbed the prince's marble steps and drank the burning potion, and a pain like a blade went through her. She fell into a faint, and when she woke, the young prince stood before her, gazing at her in wonder. Her tail was gone, replaced by slender white legs, but when he asked who she was, she could only look at him in silence. Each step felt exactly as the witch had warned, yet she bore the pain gladly, and when she danced, the whole court was enchanted. The prince declared that she must never leave him, and he let her sleep on a velvet cushion outside his door.

She rode beside him through fragrant woods and climbed the highest mountains in his company, laughing even when her delicate feet bled. At night, she would slip down the marble steps to cool her burning feet in the sea, and once her sisters rose arm in arm, singing a song of mourning. Day by day the prince grew fonder of her, yet he loved her as one loves a gentle child, never as a future queen. "You remind me of the girl from the holy house who found me on the shore," he confided, "the only one I could ever love." The little mermaid, who knew the truth and could not utter it, could only sigh without a sound.

Soon there was talk that the prince was to marry a neighboring king's daughter, and a splendid ship was made ready. He went only because his parents wished it, he insisted, but when the princess appeared, his reluctance turned to delight. She was the very girl from the white building by the shore, where she had been sent to learn the royal virtues. "It was you who saved me when I lay half dead on the sand," he cried, turning to the little mermaid to share his joy. She kissed his hand and felt her heart begin to break.

The wedding took place amid ringing bells, and that same evening the couple went aboard the ship, where a royal tent of purple and gold stood on the deck. The little mermaid danced as never before, knowing that she would not see again the man for whom she had given up her home and her voice. At midnight the couple withdrew into their tent, and she leaned on the rail, watching the east for the first red streak of dawn. Then her sisters rose out of the waves, as pale as she was, their long hair gone, for they had given it to the witch. "In return she gave us this knife," they cried, "and you must use it against the prince before the sun rises." "Then your feet will grow together into a tail, and you can live out your three hundred years with us."

She drew aside the purple curtain and saw the bride asleep beside the prince, and she heard him murmur his bride's name in his dreams. The knife trembled in her grasp; then she flung it far into the waves, which gleamed red where it fell. Once more she looked at the prince, then threw herself from the ship and felt her body dissolving into foam. Yet the sun rose warm and gentle over the water, and instead of death she felt herself drawn upward among hundreds of clear, beautiful beings. "We are the daughters of the air," they told her, "and though we have no soul either, we may earn one by doing good for three hundred years." Below, the prince and his bride were searching for her, gazing sadly at the bubbling foam as though they guessed what she had done. Unseen, she kissed the bride's forehead, smiled at the prince, and rose with the other children of the air toward a rosy cloud drifting high above the sea.
