# The Courtship of Miles Standish

Summary: A brave captain asks his young friend to carry his marriage proposal to the girl they both love.

Glossed key words (A1/A2): captain, proposal, wedding

## Level A1

A ship came to a new land. Its name was the Mayflower. The people on it made a small town. They called the town Plymouth. The first winter was very hard. Many people died.

Miles Standish was the captain of Plymouth. He was a short, strong man. But he was not good with words. His young friend John Alden lived with him. John could write very well. Every day, John wrote letters home.

One day, Standish put down his book. Standish said, "John, I want to marry Priscilla. Please take my proposal to her. I am a captain. I can't speak about love."

For a long time, John said nothing. He loved Priscilla too. But Standish was his good friend. So John said, "Yes, I can go."

Priscilla was at home. John told her about the captain's proposal. Priscilla looked at John for a long time. "Why does Standish not come?" she asked. John said many good things about Standish. Then Priscilla looked at him again. "Why don't you speak for yourself, John?"

John did not answer. He went back and told Standish everything. Standish was very angry with John. "You were my friend!" said Standish.

The next morning, Standish went far away. The Mayflower left on the same day. John wanted to go home on it. But then he saw Priscilla by the sea. So John stayed in Plymouth.

Months later, John and Priscilla had their wedding. That morning, a man came to the door. It was Miles Standish! Standish took John's hand. Then he said, "I was wrong, John. Please forget my words. Be happy together, my friends." John and Priscilla went to their new home.

## Level A2

Long ago, a small ship crossed the sea to a new land. Its name was the Mayflower. The people built a town by the sea and called it Plymouth. The first winter was long and cold. Many people died. Captain Miles Standish lost his wife, and Priscilla lost her family.

Miles Standish was the captain of Plymouth. He was a short, strong man. But he did not know how to speak about love. His young friend John Alden lived in the same house. John was kind, and he could write beautiful letters.

One day, John sat at the table and wrote letters. Standish closed his book and turned to his friend. Standish said, "John, I am alone now, and Priscilla is alone too. I want to marry her. But I am a captain, and I am not good with words. Please go to her and take my proposal."

John's face went white. He loved Priscilla too, but he never told anyone. Standish was his best friend, so John said yes. Then he walked across the fields to her house.

Priscilla sat at home and sang an old song. She smiled when she saw John at the door. Then John told her about the captain's proposal. He spoke about Standish's good heart and his strong hands. Priscilla listened, but she did not smile now. "Why does the captain not come to me himself?" she asked. "He is not good with words," said John. "A man in love must find the time," said Priscilla. Then she looked into John's eyes. "Why don't you speak for yourself, John?" she asked.

John did not answer, and he left the house quickly. He walked by the sea for a long time. He wanted to go home on the Mayflower and forget everything. In the end, he went back and told Standish all of it. The captain was very angry. Standish said, "You lived in my house and ate at my table. I was your friend, and you took her from me!" Early the next morning, Standish left Plymouth and went far away.

On the same morning, the Mayflower left for home. All the people of Plymouth came down to the water. John wanted to go with the ship. But then he saw Priscilla by the sea. He could not leave her. So John stayed, and the ship went away without him.

After that, John and Priscilla met and talked almost every day. Many months later, a man came to Plymouth with bad news. "The captain is dead," said the man. It was not true, but nobody knew it then. Soon John and Priscilla had their wedding.

On the wedding morning, a man stood at the door. It was Miles Standish! Standish took John's hand and said, "Forgive me, John. I was wrong. You are still my friend." Then the captain turned to Priscilla and said, "Be happy together." Everyone in the room laughed and talked at once. After the wedding, John and Priscilla went to their new home together.

## Level B1

Long ago, a small ship, the Mayflower, crossed the wide sea to a new land. Its people built a little town on the shore and called it Plymouth. The first winter was terrible, and cold and sickness came to almost every house. Many people died before the spring. Captain Miles Standish lost his wife that winter, and he often visited her grave. A young woman, Priscilla, lost her father, her mother and her brother.

Standish was the captain of the little town. He was short and broad, with a loud voice and a quick temper. He was afraid of nothing, but he had no talent for gentle words. His young friend John Alden shared his house. John was tall, quiet and thoughtful, and he wrote beautiful letters. The two men were very different, but they were close friends.

One spring day, John was writing letters for the Mayflower, which would sail home soon. Standish closed his book and said, "John, my house feels empty without my wife. I often think about Priscilla, and I want to make her my wife. But I am a plain captain, and I don't know any pretty words. You know books, and you know how to talk to a young woman. Please go to her and give her my proposal."

John felt a sharp pain in his heart. He loved Priscilla himself, and he dreamed of asking for her hand one day. But Standish was his oldest friend, and John could not say no to him. So he agreed, and he set out across the fields toward her house.

On the way, John picked some mayflowers for her. He found Priscilla at her spinning wheel, singing an old hymn. She was glad to see him, and she took the flowers with a smile. They talked about their old home across the sea and about the hard winter. Then John gave her the captain's proposal in a few short, awkward words.

Priscilla stopped her wheel and stared at John in surprise. "The captain wants to marry me?" asked Priscilla. "Then why doesn't he come and ask me himself?" she went on. John tried hard to help his friend. He praised Standish's courage, his honesty and his kind heart. He explained that the captain was very busy with the safety of the town. Priscilla listened, and then she smiled a little. "A man in love must find time to speak," she said. Then she looked straight at John. "Why don't you speak for yourself, John?" she asked.

John's face turned red, and he could not answer. He left the house quickly and walked along the shore for a long time. He wanted to sail away on the Mayflower and forget everything. Late that evening, he went home and told Standish the whole story. The captain's face grew dark with anger. Standish shouted, "You lived in my house and ate at my table. I trusted you, and you took her from me!" Early the next morning, the captain left Plymouth with a few men on a long journey. He did not say goodbye to John.

That same morning, the Mayflower was ready to sail home. All the people of Plymouth stood on the shore to say goodbye. John stood among them, and he wanted to step into the boat. But then he saw Priscilla's sad face in the crowd. He knew at once that he could not leave her. So he stayed, and the ship sailed away without him.

After that, John and Priscilla often met and talked as friends. John built a small house of his own in the town. Months later, a man came back from the wild country with bad news. He said that Captain Standish was dead. John and Priscilla were very sad, but now John felt free to speak. He asked Priscilla to marry him, and she said yes.

Their wedding was a simple one, on a bright morning. Friends and neighbors came to the little room. Suddenly, a man in travel clothes stood in the doorway. It was Captain Miles Standish, alive and well! Everyone stared at him in silence. Standish walked to John, took his hand and said, "Forgive me, old friend. I was angry, and I was wrong. Let us be friends again." Then the captain bowed to Priscilla and wished them both a long and happy life. The room filled with laughter, and the two old friends were together again. After the wedding, John led his bride to their new home.

## Level B2

In sixteen twenty, a small ship, the Mayflower, carried a group of families across a stormy sea to a wild land. They built their houses on a hill above the shore and named their little settlement Plymouth. The first winter was cruel. Cold and sickness went from house to house, and by spring nearly half of the settlers were gone. Among them was the young wife of Captain Miles Standish. Among them, too, were the father, mother and brother of a girl named Priscilla, who was now alone in the world.

Miles Standish was the captain of the settlement, a short, broad-shouldered man known for his quick temper and his courage. He could lead men through any danger, but a few kind words to a woman were more than he could manage. In his simple house lived John Alden, a young man with gentle manners and a gift for writing. The two men were close friends, although they were as different as fire and water.

One spring day, John sat by the window, writing letters for the Mayflower, which would soon sail back across the sea. Standish, who had been reading by the fire, suddenly closed his book and said, "John, I have a favor to ask. Since my wife died, my house has felt empty and cold. I have been thinking about Priscilla, and I want to make her my wife. But I am a rough captain, not a poet. You know books and fine words, and you know how to speak to a young woman. Go to her for me and carry my proposal."

John's pen stopped, and for a moment his heart seemed to stop with it. He had loved Priscilla in secret for months, and he had hoped one day to tell her so. Yet Standish had shared his roof, his bread and his trust, and John could not refuse his friend. He tried to make excuses, but the captain would not listen, and at last John agreed.

He walked across the fields and through the woods, struggling with his own thoughts. Along the path, mayflowers were blooming, and almost without thinking he gathered a handful for Priscilla. He found her alone at home, sitting at her spinning wheel and singing an old hymn. For a moment he simply stood at the door and listened.

Priscilla looked up, smiled, and welcomed him warmly. She took the flowers and told him how much she missed her old home across the sea. She spoke of its green hedges and its friendly neighbors. John listened kindly. Then, because he could not find a gentle way to begin, he delivered the captain's proposal in a few plain, awkward sentences.

Priscilla's wheel stopped, and for a long moment she said nothing. At last, she said, "If the captain wishes to marry me, why does he not come and tell me so himself? A man who cannot spare an hour to ask cannot care very much about the answer." John tried hard to defend his friend. He explained that the captain was busy with the safety of the town. He praised Standish's courage, honesty and good heart. Priscilla listened with a little smile, and when he had finished, she looked straight into his eyes. "Why don't you speak for yourself, John?" she asked.

John could not reply. He hurried out of the house and wandered along the beach, where the restless waves seemed to match his confusion. He decided to leave on the Mayflower the next morning, far from the woman he loved and the friend he had failed. Late that evening, he returned home and told Standish everything. The captain's face turned dark red, and he jumped to his feet. Standish cried, "You have lived under my roof and eaten at my table! I trusted you more than any man alive, and you have betrayed me!" Before John could answer, a messenger arrived to call the captain to an urgent meeting. Early the next morning, Standish left Plymouth with a small band of men on a long journey into the wild country. He did not say goodbye.

That same morning, the Mayflower was ready to sail, and the whole town gathered on the shore to watch it leave. John stood among them with his bag, and he was about to step into the boat when he turned and saw Priscilla. Her face was pale and sad, and at that moment he understood that his place was here, near her. He stepped back onto the sand, and the ship sailed away without him.

In the weeks that followed, John built a house of his own and visited Priscilla often. She told him she was sorry for speaking so boldly. They agreed to remain friends, though both of them felt something deeper. Then one day a messenger came back from the wilderness with terrible news: Captain Standish had died. The news brought grief to them both, but it also set John free. He took Priscilla's hands and told her at last how much he loved her. Then he asked her to be his wife, and she said yes.

Their wedding took place on a clear, golden morning, with a few neighbors and friends as guests. Just as the ceremony ended, a figure appeared in the doorway. To everyone's astonishment, it was Miles Standish, alive, dusty from the road and unsmiling. Nobody moved. Then the captain crossed the room, took John's hand and said, "Forgive me, John. I spoke in anger, and I was wrong. Our friendship is older and stronger than my pride." Standish turned to Priscilla with a deep bow and wished her every happiness. With a rough laugh, he added that a man should do his own asking. The room broke into joyful noise, and the two old friends embraced.

Outside, John had prepared his white bull, with a soft cloth on its back for a saddle. Priscilla rode it like a queen, while John walked beside her and led it gently by the ring in its nose. Together they went through the woods to their new home, while the whole town waved goodbye.

## Level C1

In the autumn of sixteen twenty, a small ship, the Mayflower, set out across a gray and stormy ocean, carrying a handful of families toward an unknown coast. They built their rough houses on a hill above the harbor, called their settlement Plymouth, and prepared, as best they could, for the winter ahead. It proved crueler than anyone had feared. Cold and sickness moved from door to door, and by the time the first green appeared in the fields, nearly half of the settlers had been laid to rest on the hill. Among them was the young wife of Captain Miles Standish. Among them, too, were the father, mother and brother of a girl named Priscilla, who now faced the new world entirely alone.

Miles Standish was the captain of the little colony, a short, broad-shouldered man with a ruddy face and a temper that flared as quickly as it cooled. No danger in the forest could make him hesitate, yet the thought of offering a few tender words to a woman filled him with a kind of dread. Sharing his plain wooden house was John Alden, a fair-haired young man of gentle manners, whose pen was as skilled as the captain's tongue was clumsy. They were as unlike as fire and water, and perhaps for that very reason they had become the closest of friends.

One spring day, John sat at the window, filling page after page with letters for the Mayflower, which was soon to make the long voyage home. Standish, who had been reading an old history by the hearth, closed the volume with a snap and said, "John, there is something I must ask of you. Since my wife died, this house has felt as empty as a winter field. I have been thinking a great deal about Priscilla, and I believe she would make me a good and loyal wife. But I am a rough captain, not a poet, and I would rather face a storm at sea than stand before a young woman with my hat in my hand. You have read every book in this colony, and you know how such things are said. Go to her on my behalf and carry my proposal."

John's pen stopped in the middle of a word, and for a moment it seemed that his heart had stopped as well. He had loved Priscilla in silence for months, cherishing the hope that one day he would find the courage to tell her so. Yet this was the man who had shared his roof, his bread and his trust, and to refuse him now felt like a kind of betrayal. He stammered a few excuses, which the captain waved away with a laugh, and in the end John took up his hat and went.

The path to Priscilla's house ran across open fields and through a wood where the first mayflowers were blooming among last year's leaves. John walked slowly, arguing with himself at every step, and almost without noticing he gathered a handful of the pale blossoms. Long before he reached her door he could hear her voice, for Priscilla sat at her spinning wheel, singing an old hymn as the thread ran through her fingers. He stood on the threshold and listened, unwilling to break the spell.

When she looked up and saw him, her face brightened, and she welcomed him with unaffected warmth. She took the flowers, held them to her face, and spoke of the home she had left across the sea, of green hedges and lanes full of familiar faces. Beside all that, she confessed, the bare hills of Plymouth seemed very lonely. John listened with an aching heart. Unable to find any graceful way to begin, he delivered the captain's proposal in a handful of blunt, awkward sentences, like a man dropping a load too heavy to carry.

The spinning wheel fell silent, and for a long moment Priscilla said nothing at all. Then she said, quietly but firmly, "If the great captain is so eager to marry me, why has he not come to tell me so himself? A man who cannot spare one hour to ask a question can hardly care very much about the answer." John, loyal to the last, tried to defend his friend. He spoke of the captain's endless duties, of his courage, his honesty and the warm heart hidden beneath his rough manner, and the more he praised Standish, the more eloquent he became. Priscilla listened with a small smile playing at the corners of her mouth, and when at last he paused for breath, she looked straight into his eyes. "Why don't you speak for yourself, John?" she asked.

John had no answer. He stumbled out of the house and wandered along the beach until dusk, the restless waves echoing the confusion inside him. Somewhere on that gray shore he resolved to sail on the Mayflower in the morning, putting an ocean between himself and everything he had lost. Late that evening he returned home and told Standish everything, holding nothing back. The captain's face darkened to a deep red, and he sprang to his feet. Standish cried, "You have lived under my roof and eaten at my table! I trusted you more than any man alive, and this is how you repay me!" Before John could say a word in his own defense, a messenger knocked at the door to summon the captain to an urgent council. At first light the next morning, Standish left Plymouth with a small band of men on a long journey into the wild country, and he did not look back to say goodbye.

That same morning the Mayflower lay ready to sail, and the whole settlement gathered on the shore to watch the last link with the old world depart. John stood among them with his bag over his shoulder, and he was on the very point of stepping into the boat when he turned and caught sight of Priscilla. Her face was pale, her eyes fixed on him, and in that instant he knew with perfect certainty that his place was here and nowhere else. He stepped back onto the wet sand, and the ship spread its sails and slipped away without him.

The weeks that followed passed quietly. John built a small house of his own at the edge of the town and called on Priscilla often, and she, in turn, apologized for having spoken so boldly that afternoon. They agreed to remain friends, though neither of them was entirely deceived by the word. Then, one evening, a messenger returned from the wilderness with grave news: Captain Standish was dead. The news filled them both with sorrow, yet it also lifted the weight of an old promise from John's shoulders. Taking Priscilla's hands in his, he told her at last everything he had kept hidden for so long. Then he asked her to be his wife, and she said yes without a moment's hesitation.

Their wedding took place on a clear and golden morning, in the presence of a few neighbors and friends. Scarcely had the final words of the ceremony been spoken when a figure appeared in the open doorway. To the astonishment of everyone present, it was Miles Standish himself, alive, dusty from the road and entirely unsmiling. For a long moment nobody in the room dared to move. Then the captain strode across the floor, seized John's hand and said, "Forgive me, John. I spoke in anger, and I have regretted it every day since. Our friendship is older and stronger than my pride, and I will not let a few hot words destroy it." Standish turned to the bride with a deep bow and wished her every happiness. A man who wants a thing done properly, he remarked with a rough laugh, ought to do his own asking. At that, the room broke into joyful noise, and the two old friends embraced like brothers.

Outside, John had prepared his snow-white bull, laying a soft cloth across its broad back in place of a saddle. Priscilla rode it like a queen through the crowd of well-wishers, while John walked beside her, leading the gentle animal by the ring in its nose. Together they passed into the shade of the woods and on toward their new home, as the whole town stood at the edge of the clearing and waved them out of sight.
