# Thor's Journey to Utgard

Summary: Thor and his friends visit a castle of giants and take part in some very strange contests.

Glossed key words (A1/A2): giant, horn, contest, hammer

## Level A1

Thor had a big, strong hammer. One day, he went far away with Loki. Two children came too, Thialfi and Roskva. They went to the land of the giants.

At night, they found a big house. They went in and slept. In the night, the land moved under them. There was a big sound too.

In the morning, Thor went outside. A giant was there, very near the house. The sound came from him. "My name is Skrymir," said the giant. He took the house in his hand. It was his glove!

Skrymir showed them the way to Utgard. Skrymir said, "The giants there are very big. Do not tell them about your power."

Utgard was a big castle. The biggest giant was Utgard-Loki. He looked at Thor and laughed. "Is this little man really Thor? What can you do?"

"I eat very fast," said Loki. Loki and a giant, Logi, sat down. A long box of food was between them. Loki ate only the food. Logi ate the food and the box! So Loki lost.

Hugi was a young giant. Thialfi ran three races with him. Hugi won every time.

Utgard-Loki gave Thor a long horn. "Drink all the water in it," he said. Thor tried three times. But the horn was still almost full. Next, Thor tried to lift a big cat. But only one of its feet went up. Then an old woman, Elli, came in. Thor tried to push her down. But Thor went down on one knee!

Next day, Utgard-Loki went out with them. "Now I can tell you everything," he said. "I was Skrymir. All the contests were magic. Logi was fire, and fire eats everything. Hugi was a thought, and thoughts are fast. The horn's end was in the sea. The cat was a very big snake. It goes around the whole world. And Elli was old age. Nobody can win against old age." Thor took his hammer in his hand. But the giant and his castle were gone.

## Level A2

Thor was very strong, and he always carried a big hammer. One day he went to the land of the giants. His friend Loki went with him. Two children, Thialfi and Roskva, came too.

They walked through a dark forest all day. At night, they found a strange house with a very wide door. They went inside and lay down on the floor. In the middle of the night, the ground moved under them. Everyone went into a small room at the side.

In the morning, Thor went outside. A big giant lay there near the house. The sound in the night came from him. "I am Skrymir," said the giant. He picked up the house from the ground. The house was his glove, and the small room was its thumb!

Skrymir walked with them and carried their food in his bag. At night, he went to sleep under a tree. Thor wanted to eat, but he could not open the bag. So everyone went to sleep without any food.

In the morning, Skrymir showed them the way to Utgard. Skrymir said, "The giants there are much bigger than me. Do not tell them about your power." Then he went away into the forest.

Utgard was a very high castle. The gate was closed. But the friends were small, so they went in between its bars. Inside, many giants sat on long seats. Their leader, Utgard-Loki, looked at Thor and laughed. "Is this little man really the great Thor? What can you do?"

"I can eat faster than anyone," said Loki. So Utgard-Loki called one of his men, Logi. The giants put a long box of meat on the floor. Loki and Logi ate from the two ends. Loki ate all the meat on his side. But Logi ate the meat, the bones and the box too!

Next, Thialfi ran a race against a young man, Hugi. They ran three times, and Hugi won every time.

"Now it is your turn, Thor," said Utgard-Loki. He gave Thor a long horn full of water. "A good man drinks all of it at once," he said. Thor drank and drank, three times. But the horn was still almost full. Next, Thor tried to lift Utgard-Loki's cat. But only one of the cat's feet left the floor. Last, an old woman called Elli came in. Thor and Elli pushed hard against each other. Elli did not move, and Thor went down on one knee.

The next morning, Utgard-Loki walked with them out of the castle. "Now I can tell you everything," he said. "I was Skrymir in the forest. I closed my bag with magic. Logi was fire, and fire eats everything. Hugi was my thought, and nothing is faster than a thought. The end of your horn was in the sea. Now the sea is lower because of you. My cat was the great snake around the whole world. And Elli was old age, and old age wins against everyone. You are very strong, Thor, so please never come back." Thor took his hammer in his hand. But the giant and his castle were gone.

## Level B1

Thor was the strongest of the gods, and he never went anywhere without his hammer. One day he decided to travel east to the land of the giants. Loki, who was clever and full of tricks, came with him. They also brought two young helpers, a brother and sister called Thialfi and Roskva.

They crossed the sea and walked into a huge, dark forest. When night came, they were tired and looking for a place to rest. At last, they found a strange building with a door as wide as the whole house. They went inside and lay down to sleep. In the middle of the night, the ground shook. A loud noise came out of the dark. Everyone hurried into a smaller room on one side and waited there. Thor sat by the door all night, holding his hammer.

In the morning Thor stepped outside and saw a giant sleeping under the trees. The terrible noise was only his snoring. When the giant woke up, he told them his name was Skrymir. Then he looked around and asked, "Where is my glove?" He bent down and picked up the building where they spent the night. It was his glove, and the little side room was its thumb.

Skrymir offered to travel with them and carry their food in his own bag. That evening he lay down under an oak tree and fell asleep at once. Thor tried to open the bag for dinner, but he could not untie a single knot. He worked at it until his face was red, and then he gave up. The four friends went to bed without any food.

The next morning Skrymir showed them the road to Utgard. "You think I am big," he said, "but the men in Utgard are much bigger. Do not boast in front of them, or they will not be kind to you." Then he turned and walked away into the forest.

By noon, they reached Utgard, a castle that seemed to touch the sky. The gate was locked, but they squeezed through the bars. Inside, giants were sitting on long benches in a great hall. Their king, Utgard-Loki, looked at Thor and laughed. "Is this tiny fellow really the famous Thor?" he said. "Everyone who stays here must be very good at something. What can you do?"

"I can eat faster than anyone in this hall," said Loki. Utgard-Loki called one of his men, Logi. Then the servants brought a long wooden trough full of meat. Loki sat at one end and Logi at the other, and they started to eat. They met exactly in the middle. Loki ate every piece of meat on his half. But Logi ate the meat, the bones and even the wooden trough. Everyone agreed that Loki lost.

Then Thialfi ran a race against a young man called Hugi. They raced three times across a long field. Each time, Hugi was already waiting at the end when Thialfi arrived.

"Now it is your turn, Thor," said Utgard-Loki. A servant brought a long horn full of water. "A strong man empties this horn in one drink," said the giant king. "Most of my men need two drinks, and nobody needs three." Thor drank for as long as he could, three times. Yet when he looked into the horn, the water was only a little lower.

"Then try something easy," said Utgard-Loki. "Lift my gray cat from the floor." Thor put his arm under the cat's belly and pushed up with all his strength. The cat only arched its back, and in the end just one paw left the floor. Thor was angry now, and he asked for a wrestling match. Utgard-Loki called his old nurse, Elli. The harder Thor pushed, the more firmly the old woman stood. In the end Thor went down onto one knee, and the king stopped the contest.

In the morning Utgard-Loki walked with them out of the castle. "Now that you are outside, I will tell you the truth," he said. "I was Skrymir, the giant in the forest. I tied my bag with magic iron, so you could not open it. Logi was fire itself, which can eat wood and bones and everything else. Hugi was my thought, and no one can run as fast as thought. The end of your horn was in the sea. Go and look, and you will see how much lower the sea is now. The cat was the great serpent that lies around the whole world. When you lifted its paw, we were all afraid. And Elli was old age, and in the end nobody wins against old age. Do not come back here, because I will always use magic to keep you away." Thor reached for his hammer, but the giant was gone. Where the castle once stood, there was only an open, empty field.

## Level B2

Thor, the strongest of the gods, was famous for his hammer, which no giant could stand against. One spring he decided to travel east, into the cold country where the giants lived. Loki, who was quick with words and even quicker with tricks, came along for the adventure. With them went two young helpers, a farmer's son called Thialfi and his sister Roskva. Thialfi could run faster than anyone in the world of men, and Roskva was brave and patient.

They crossed a deep sea and then walked for a long time through a huge, silent forest. By nightfall, they were tired, and they began to look for somewhere to rest. At last, they came upon a strange building, and its doorway was as wide as the hall itself. Too tired to wonder about it, they lay down on the floor and fell asleep. Around midnight, however, the ground began to shake, and a deep rumbling sound rolled through the dark. The travelers found a smaller room on one side and crowded into it. Thor sat in the doorway until dawn, holding his hammer and listening.

When the sky grew light, Thor went outside and saw what had made the noise. A giant was lying asleep among the trees, and the rumbling was simply his snoring. As Thor watched, the giant woke, sat up and told them that his name was Skrymir. "I know who you are," he added, looking down at Thor, "but where has my glove gone?" He reached over and picked up the building where they had spent the night. It was his glove, and the little side room had been its thumb.

Skrymir offered to travel with them, and he put all their food into his own great bag. He walked with such long steps that the others had to hurry to keep up. That evening he lay down under a tall oak, told them to help themselves to supper, and began to snore at once. Thor tried to open the bag, but the knots would not move, however hard he pulled. In the end he gave up, and the four companions went to sleep hungry.

In the morning Skrymir pointed out the road to the castle of Utgard. "I have heard you whispering that I am big," he said, "but in Utgard you will meet far bigger men. Do not boast there, because Utgard-Loki and his people will not accept proud words from small fellows." With that, he threw his bag over his shoulder and disappeared among the trees.

At midday, they reached Utgard, a castle so high that they had to bend their heads right back to see the top. The gate was locked, so they slipped in between the bars. In a vast hall, rows of giants were sitting on long benches, and their king, Utgard-Loki, sat among them. He looked at Thor for a long moment and then smiled with open scorn. "Can this little fellow really be the mighty Thor?" he asked. "Perhaps you are stronger than you look. Nobody may stay with us unless he is a master of some skill, so tell me what you can do."

Loki, who was very hungry, spoke first. "I can eat faster than anyone in this hall," he said. "We shall soon see," replied the king, and he called a man named Logi from the far end of the bench. A long wooden trough piled with meat was set on the floor, with Loki at one end and Logi at the other. Each ate as fast as he could, and they met exactly in the middle. Loki had eaten all the meat on his side, but Logi had eaten the meat, the bones and the trough as well. Everyone could see that Loki had lost.

Next, Thialfi offered to run a race. Utgard-Loki chose a slim young man called Hugi, and the two ran three times across a level field. In the first race Hugi reached the finish so far ahead that he turned around to greet Thialfi. In the second and third, the distance between them only grew.

"Now, Thor," said the king, "show us what made you famous." A servant brought a long drinking horn filled with water. "A good man empties this horn in one drink," Utgard-Loki explained. "A weaker man needs two, but nobody here needs three." Thor lifted the horn and drank until his breath ran out. When he looked, the water had hardly gone down. He tried a second time and then a third, yet the horn was still almost full.

"You are not as strong as people say," said the king. "Our young men like to lift my gray cat from the floor. Try that, if you think it is not too hard for you." Thor put his arm under the cat's belly and pushed upward with all his strength. The cat simply arched its back higher and higher, and in the end only one of its paws left the ground. Red with shame, Thor demanded a wrestling match. The king laughed and called for his old nurse, Elli, who came in slowly without a stick. The harder Thor pushed, the more firmly she stood, and at last he went down onto one knee. Utgard-Loki ended the contest there, and the guests were given a fine supper and beds for the night.

In the morning the king walked with them out through the gate. "Now that you are outside my castle, I will tell you the truth," he said. "If I had known your strength, I would never have let you in. I was Skrymir, and I tied my bag with magic iron, so no hand could open it. Logi was wildfire, which eats wood, bone and everything in its path. Hugi was my own thought, and no runner can ever be faster than thought. The end of your horn reached down into the sea. When you reach the shore, you will see how much the water has dropped. The cat was the great serpent that lies around the whole world. When you lifted it so high, we were all afraid. And Elli was old age, which in the end brings every person down. It was a wonder that you fell no further than one knee. Do not come back, because I will always guard my castle with tricks like these." Thor reached for his hammer, but the king had vanished. Where the castle had stood, there was nothing but an empty, wide field.

## Level C1

Of all the gods, none was stronger than Thor, and none was more certain of it, for he carried a hammer that no giant had ever been able to stand against. It was perhaps this certainty that sent him one spring on a long journey east, into the cold country where the giants made their homes. Loki went with him, partly for the adventure and partly because a journey without mischief held little interest for him. They were joined by two young helpers, a farmer's son named Thialfi and his sister Roskva, who carried what they could and complained about nothing. Thialfi was famous among men for his speed, and few could keep pace with him for long.

The travelers crossed a deep stretch of sea and then walked for a whole day through a forest so vast and silent that even Loki stopped talking. By nightfall, they were tired and hungry, and they began searching among the trees for some kind of shelter. What they found was a strange, empty hall, whose doorway was as broad as the building itself. Too weary to ask who had built such a place, they lay down on its floor and were soon asleep. Around midnight the ground began to tremble, and a deep, rolling rumble came out of the darkness, rising and falling like distant thunder. Feeling their way along the wall, the companions discovered a narrow side chamber and crowded into it, while Thor sat in the doorway with his hammer across his knees until dawn.

In the pale light of morning, he stepped outside and found the source of the noise. A giant lay sprawled among the trees, and the rumble that had shaken the ground was nothing more than his snoring. As Thor stood looking at him, the giant opened his eyes, sat up, and announced that his name was Skrymir. "I need not ask who you are, little Thor," he went on, glancing about him, "but tell me, have you seen my glove?" He stretched out a hand and lifted up the very hall in which they had slept, and only then did they understand that the side chamber had been its thumb.

Skrymir suggested that they travel together, and before anyone could object, he had tipped all their provisions into his own enormous bag. He walked with such long strides that the others had to run to keep him in sight. When evening came, he stretched out beneath a great oak, told them cheerfully to help themselves to supper, and was snoring again within moments. Thor set to work on the bag, but however hard he pulled, not a single knot gave way. At last, with an aching back and an empty stomach, he gave up, and the four companions settled down to a hungry night.

At first light Skrymir pointed out the road to Utgard and offered them a piece of advice along with it. "I heard you whispering that I was rather large," he said, "but in Utgard you will find men far bigger than I am. Keep your proud words to yourselves there, because Utgard-Loki and his people have little patience with boasting from small fellows." Having said this, he slung the bag over his shoulder and strode off into the forest, and the travelers were not sorry to see him go.

They reached Utgard at midday, and the castle rose so high above them that they had to tip their heads right back to see the top of its walls. Finding the gate locked and nobody willing to open it, they simply slipped in between the bars, which stood far enough apart for creatures of their modest size. Inside a vast hall, rows of giants sat on long benches, and at their center sat the king, Utgard-Loki. He let the newcomers wait a long while before he deigned to notice them. "Can this small fellow truly be the mighty Thor?" he said at last, with an unpleasant smile. "Perhaps there is more to you than meets the eye. In any case, no one may stay among us who is not a master of some skill, so tell me what you and your companions are able to do."

Loki, whose stomach had been empty since the forest, answered first. "There is nobody in this hall who can eat faster than I can," he declared. "That claim is easily tested," replied the king, and he summoned a man called Logi from the far end of the bench. A long wooden trough heaped with meat was placed on the floor, with Loki at one end and Logi at the other, and at a signal both began to eat. They met precisely in the middle, but whereas Loki had cleaned the meat from every bone on his side, Logi had swallowed meat, bones and trough alike. There was no question about the result, and Loki returned to his seat in an unusually quiet mood.

Thialfi stepped forward next and offered to run against anyone the king might choose. Utgard-Loki picked a slender young man named Hugi, and the two raced three times along a level course outside the walls. In the first race Hugi crossed the finish so far ahead that he had time to turn around and wait for his rival. The second and third races only widened the gap, until even the giants began to look sorry for the boy.

"And now, Thor," said the king, leaning forward, "we come to you, whose deeds are sung about so loudly." A servant carried in a long drinking horn brimming with water, and Utgard-Loki explained the custom of the house. "A good man empties this horn in a single drink, an ordinary one needs two, but no one here is so feeble that he needs three." Thor raised the horn and drank until his breath gave out, yet when he lowered it, the water had scarcely moved. He tried a second time, and then a third, and the horn was still very nearly full.

"You are clearly less than the stories claim," remarked the king, not unkindly. "Our boys amuse themselves by lifting my gray cat off the floor; perhaps you might manage that." Thor slid his arm under the cat's belly and heaved upward with all the strength he possessed, but the animal merely arched its back higher and higher. Stretch as he might, he could not raise more than a single paw from the ground. Burning with shame, Thor challenged anyone in the hall to wrestle him, and the king, laughing, sent for his old nurse Elli. The bent old woman tottered in without a stick, yet the harder Thor strained against her, the more firmly she held her ground. After a long struggle Thor sank onto one knee, and Utgard-Loki declared the contest over before anything worse could happen. The guests were then treated with great courtesy, given a generous supper and shown to comfortable beds, which somehow made the evening harder to bear.

Next morning the king himself walked them out through the gate, and only when they stood on open ground did he speak plainly. "Now that you are outside my walls, you shall have the truth," he said, "and had I known your strength beforehand, you would never have come inside them. I was Skrymir in the forest, and I had tied my bag with magic iron, so that no hand on earth could have opened it. Logi was wildfire, which devours wood and bone and the trough beneath them with equal appetite. Hugi was my own thought, and no runner, however gifted, can ever outpace thought. The far end of your horn lay in the sea itself, and when you reach the shore, you will see how much the water has fallen because of your drinking. The cat was the great serpent that lies coiled around the whole world, and when you lifted it so close to the sky, every one of us shook with fear. As for Elli, she was old age, which sooner or later brings every living person to the ground, and it was a marvel that you went down no further than one knee. So we shall part now, and you will not find me again, because I shall always defend my castle with tricks of this kind." Thor reached for his hammer, but by the time his hand closed around it, the king was gone. He turned toward Utgard, meaning to speak with its people, yet where the castle had stood there was nothing but a wide, empty plain under the morning sky.
