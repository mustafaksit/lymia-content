# Doctor Ox's Experiment

Summary: A strange doctor brings new pipes to a sleepy Flemish town, and the people begin to change.

Glossed key words (A1/A2): oxygen, gas, experiment

## Level A1

Quiquendone was a small town in Flanders. Life there was very slow. The people did everything slowly. No one ever ran in the town. A small problem took ten years to decide.

Van Tricasse was the head of the town. His friend Niklausse helped him with his work. The two men talked for hours every day. But they never decided anything.

Then a man came to Quiquendone. His name was Doctor Ox. A young man, Ygene, worked with him. Doctor Ox went to see Van Tricasse. "I want to give your town light. It comes from gas, and it is free." Van Tricasse liked the plan and said yes.

Doctor Ox built a gas house. His men put pipes into every house. But Doctor Ox had another plan. He talked about it with Ygene. "The pipes do not bring light. They bring oxygen into the town. Oxygen can change people. This is my experiment." No one in the town knew about it.

Soon the people of Quiquendone changed. Now they ran from place to place. They all talked at the same time. Old friends started to argue. The plants grew very big.

Van Tricasse and Niklausse changed too. Then they argued about a small thing. They shouted at each other for an hour. "You are not right!" said Van Tricasse. "No, you are not right!" said Niklausse.

The town had a very high tower. One morning, the two friends went up. There was little oxygen up there. So the two men felt calm again. They shook hands. But then they went down. In the town, they started to argue again.

Then the whole town heard a loud sound. Doctor Ox's gas house exploded. Everyone was safe. But now the pipes did not bring oxygen.

Soon the people were slow again. Van Tricasse and Niklausse were good friends again. Doctor Ox and Ygene left the town. No one in Quiquendone knew the reason. And life there was slow once more.

## Level A2

Quiquendone was a small town in Flanders. Life there was very slow. The people walked slowly, talked slowly and even thought slowly. No one ever ran in the streets, and no one ever argued. A small problem often took ten years to decide. The people of the town liked their life this way.

Van Tricasse was the head of the town. His old friend Niklausse helped him with his work. Every day, the two men sat together and talked for hours. They talked about many problems, but they never decided anything. "We must not decide too fast," Van Tricasse often said.

Then a man from far away came to Quiquendone. His name was Doctor Ox, and he was a man of science. A young man worked with him, and his name was Ygene. Doctor Ox went to see Van Tricasse with an offer. "I want to give your town light from gas. Every house can have light at night. You do not need to pay for it." Van Tricasse liked the offer, so he said yes.

Doctor Ox built a big gas house near the town. His men put pipes from it into every house and building. But Doctor Ox did not tell the town his real plan. He only told Ygene, because Ygene worked with him. "These pipes do not bring light," said Doctor Ox. "They put oxygen into the air. Oxygen can make people and plants change. I want to see this with my own eyes. This is my experiment." No one in the town knew about this plan.

Soon the people of Quiquendone began to change. Now they ran in the streets and talked very fast. They all wanted to speak at the same time. Old friends started to argue about small things. In the gardens, the plants grew very big.

Van Tricasse and Niklausse changed too. Now they decided many things in one short meeting. Then one day they argued about a very small problem. They shouted at each other for an hour. "You are not right!" said Van Tricasse. "No, you are not right!" said Niklausse. For the first time, the two old friends did not agree.

The town had a very high tower in the middle of it. One morning, Van Tricasse and Niklausse went up the tower. At the top, there was not much oxygen in the air. Suddenly the two old friends felt calm, and they shook hands. But back in the streets, they started to argue again.

Then the people heard a loud sound from outside the town. Doctor Ox's gas house exploded. Everyone was safe, but the gas house was gone. Now the pipes did not bring oxygen into the air.

Soon the people of Quiquendone were slow again. Van Tricasse and Niklausse stopped their argument and became good friends again. Doctor Ox and Ygene left the town and never came back. No one in Quiquendone ever knew the reason for the change. And life there was slow once more.

## Level B1

Quiquendone was a small town in Flanders, and it was the slowest town in the world. Its people walked slowly, spoke slowly and, most of all, made decisions slowly. Nobody ever ran in the streets, and nobody ever raised their voice. A small question about a bridge or a road could take ten years to answer. The people did not mind this at all, because they loved their quiet life.

The head of the town was Van Tricasse, a calm man who never hurried. His old friend Niklausse helped him to rule the town. Every day, the two men sat together for hours and discussed the town's problems. They discussed everything carefully, but they never actually decided anything. "We must never act too fast," Van Tricasse liked to say, and Niklausse always agreed.

Then a stranger arrived in Quiquendone. He was a man of science called Doctor Ox. He had a young assistant named Ygene. Doctor Ox visited Van Tricasse with a surprising offer. "I would like to bring light to your town," said Doctor Ox. "It will come from gas, and every street will be bright at night. And your town will not pay anything for it." Van Tricasse and Niklausse discussed the offer for a long time. In the end they said yes, because the light was free.

Doctor Ox built a large gas house just outside the town. His workers laid pipes from the gas house to every home and every building. But Doctor Ox did not tell the town his real plan. He only shared it with Ygene, who worked by his side every day. "These pipes will not bring light, at least not yet," said Doctor Ox. "They will fill the air of the town with pure oxygen. I want to see what oxygen does to people, animals and plants. This whole town is my experiment." Ygene agreed to keep the secret, so nobody in Quiquendone knew the truth.

Soon the people of Quiquendone began to change. They walked fast, talked fast and wanted everything at once. At the theater, the singers sang so quickly that a long show ended in one hour. Old friends started to argue about small things in the street. In the gardens, the plants grew huge, and the animals on the farms became very large.

Van Tricasse and Niklausse were changing too. Now they made ten decisions in one short meeting. Then one day they argued about a very small question. They shouted at each other for an hour, and their faces turned red. "You are wrong, and you are always slow!" cried Van Tricasse. "No, you are the one who is wrong!" cried Niklausse. For the first time in their lives, the two old friends were angry with each other.

Soon the whole town was angry about something. Long ago, a cow from the next town ate grass in Quiquendone. Suddenly everyone remembered this old problem. The people argued loudly about it for days. At last, they decided to walk to the next town and argue with its people there.

The town had a very high tower in its center. One morning, before the walk began, Van Tricasse and Niklausse climbed the tower. At the top, the air was clean, because the heavy oxygen stayed down in the streets. Suddenly the two men felt calm, and they shook hands like old friends. But as soon as they came down, they started to argue again.

Then a huge sound came from outside the town. Doctor Ox's gas house exploded. Everyone was safe, but the gas house was gone, and the pipes stopped bringing oxygen.

Within a few hours, the people of Quiquendone were slow again. Nobody wanted to argue with the next town anymore, so the walk never happened. Van Tricasse and Niklausse forgot their argument and became good friends again. Doctor Ox and Ygene left the town and never came back. Nobody in Quiquendone ever learned the reason for those strange days. And life in the slowest town in the world was quiet once more.

## Level B2

Quiquendone was a small town in Flanders, and it was almost certainly the slowest town in the world. Its people walked slowly and spoke slowly. Above all, they made decisions so slowly that a single question could outlive the person who first asked it. Nobody had ever been seen running in its streets, and nobody had ever heard a raised voice there. A plan to repair a bridge might be discussed for ten years before anyone touched a stone. At night the streets were dark, because the town had never found the time to decide on lamps. The people did not complain, because they believed that a calm life was the best life of all.

The head of the town was Van Tricasse, a calm and careful man who had never hurried in his life. His old friend Niklausse helped him to govern, and the two of them met every day to discuss the town's affairs. They would sit for hours with a jug of water between them and examine every problem from every side. At the end of each meeting, however, nothing had been decided. Van Tricasse believed that an open question could do no harm, while a hasty answer could do a great deal. "We must never act in haste," Van Tricasse liked to say, and Niklausse always nodded slowly in agreement.

Into this peaceful place came a stranger. He was a man of science who called himself Doctor Ox, and he brought with him a young assistant named Ygene. Soon after he arrived, Doctor Ox visited Van Tricasse with an offer that seemed too good to refuse. "I would like to light your town with gas," Doctor Ox explained. "Every street, every home and every public building will be bright at night. I will pay for everything myself, and your town will not spend a single coin." Van Tricasse and Niklausse discussed the offer for many weeks, as they discussed everything. In the end they accepted it, because the light was free and the town had nothing to lose.

Doctor Ox built a large gas house just outside the town, and his workers laid pipes to every home and every building. Yet the doctor had not told the town his real purpose. He shared it only with Ygene, who worked beside him and had to know every detail. "These pipes will not carry light, at least not yet," Doctor Ox told his assistant one evening. "They will fill the air of Quiquendone with pure oxygen. I want to learn what oxygen does to people, to animals and to plants. I chose Quiquendone because no town is calmer, so every change will be easy to see. The whole town is my experiment, and nobody here must know." Ygene promised to keep the secret, so the people of Quiquendone went on believing that the pipes were for light.

It did not take long for the town to change. People who had always strolled now hurried, and people who had always whispered now spoke at the tops of their voices. At the theater, a long show that usually lasted all evening was over in one hour. The singers simply raced through every song, and the audience did not mind at all. Old friends began to argue in the street about things that had never troubled them before. In the gardens the plants grew enormous, and on the farms the animals became surprisingly large.

Van Tricasse and Niklausse were changing too, although neither of them noticed it. Their meetings, which had once lasted all day, now produced ten decisions in a few minutes. Then one afternoon they argued about a very small question, and the argument grew louder and louder. They shouted at each other for an hour, until both their faces were red. "You are wrong, and you have always been too slow!" cried Van Tricasse. "No, you are the one who is wrong!" cried Niklausse. For the first time in their long friendship, the two men parted in anger.

Before long the whole town was angry about something. Someone remembered an old story about a cow from the next town. Long ago, the cow had eaten grass in Quiquendone, and suddenly this ancient matter seemed very important. The people argued loudly about it for days. At last, they decided to march to the next town and argue with its people face to face.

The town had a very high tower in its center. One morning, before the march began, Van Tricasse and Niklausse climbed the tower to look out over the land. From up there they could see the fields and the road to the next town. At the top, where the heavy oxygen could not reach, the air was clean and ordinary. Little by little the two men grew calm, and before long they were shaking hands like the old friends they were. Yet as soon as they came down into the streets, the quarrel began again.

Then a tremendous sound came from outside the town. Doctor Ox's gas house had exploded. Everyone was safe, but the building was gone, and the pipes no longer carried any oxygen into the town. All over the town, people stopped shouting and looked at one another in surprise.

Within a few hours, the people of Quiquendone were as slow as they had always been. Nobody wanted to argue with the next town anymore, so the march never took place. Van Tricasse and Niklausse forgot their quarrel and became good friends again. They returned to their long daily meetings, where once again nothing was ever decided. Doctor Ox and Ygene left the town quietly and were never seen there again. Nobody in Quiquendone ever learned the true reason for those strange days. And life in the slowest town in the world became calm once more.

## Level C1

Quiquendone was a small town in Flanders, and if there was a slower town anywhere in the world, nobody had ever managed to find it. Its people walked slowly, spoke slowly and, above all, made decisions with such care that a single question could easily outlive the citizen who had first raised it. No one had ever been seen running through its narrow streets, and a raised voice was something that the oldest inhabitants could not remember hearing. A proposal to repair a bridge might be debated for ten years before a single stone was touched, and nobody thought this in the least unusual. The streets, for their part, lay in darkness every night, since the question of lamps had been under discussion for longer than anyone could say. Far from complaining, the people of Quiquendone regarded their calm as a kind of wisdom, and they pitied the restless towns around them.

The head of the town was Van Tricasse, a calm and careful man who had never hurried in his life and saw no reason to begin. His oldest friend, Niklausse, helped him to govern, and the two of them met every day to consider the affairs of the town. They would sit for hours with nothing before them but a jug of water, turning each problem over and over as if it were a precious stone. At the end of each meeting, however, nothing had ever been decided, and both men went home perfectly satisfied. "We must never act in haste," Van Tricasse liked to say, and Niklausse, after a suitable pause, would always nod in agreement.

Into this peaceful place came a stranger, and with him came the first surprise that Quiquendone had known in centuries. He was a man of science who called himself Doctor Ox, and he was accompanied by a young assistant named Ygene, as quick and quiet as his master was bold. Not long after his arrival, Doctor Ox called on Van Tricasse with an offer that seemed almost too generous to be real. "I should like to light your town with gas," Doctor Ox explained with a smile. "Every street, every home and every public building will shine at night as brightly as at noon. The entire cost will be mine, and your town will not pay a single coin." Van Tricasse and Niklausse weighed the offer for many weeks, exactly as they weighed everything. In the end they accepted it, reasoning that the light was free and that a town with nothing to pay had nothing to lose.

Doctor Ox built a large gas house just outside the town, and his workers laid pipes to every home, every shop and every public building. What the doctor had not told the town, however, was his real purpose. He shared it with Ygene alone, since his assistant worked beside him day and night and could hardly avoid learning every detail. "These pipes will not carry light, at least not yet," Doctor Ox told him one evening, when the workers had gone home. "They will fill the air of Quiquendone with pure oxygen, a little more each day. I mean to discover what oxygen does to men, to animals and to growing things, and I could not wish for a calmer place to begin. The whole town is my experiment, and nobody here must suspect a thing." Ygene promised to keep the secret, and so the citizens of Quiquendone went on believing that the pipes in their walls were meant for nothing more than light.

The change, when it came, came quickly. People who had always strolled now hurried, and people who had always murmured now spoke at the tops of their voices. At the theater, a long show that usually filled an entire evening was over in a single hour, since the singers raced through every song as though pursued. Old friends began to quarrel in the street over matters that had never troubled them before, and shopkeepers argued with customers about the price of bread. Even the town council, which had never finished anything, now made new rules every morning and changed them again every afternoon. In the gardens, the plants grew to enormous size, while on the farms the animals became so large that their owners hardly recognized them.

Van Tricasse and Niklausse were changing too, although neither of them noticed it, since each was busy noticing the faults of the other. Their meetings, which had once lasted from morning until evening, now produced ten decisions in a few breathless minutes. Then one afternoon they disagreed about a question so small that neither could afterwards remember what it was, and the argument grew louder with every sentence. For a full hour they shouted at each other across the table, until both their faces were as red as apples. "You are wrong, and you have always been hopelessly slow!" cried Van Tricasse. "No, it is you who are wrong, and you always were!" cried Niklausse. For the first time in a friendship of many decades, the two men parted in anger.

Before long, the whole town was angry about something, and it was not difficult to find a target. Someone recalled that, centuries earlier, a cow from the next town had wandered across the border and eaten grass in Quiquendone, and this ancient injustice suddenly seemed intolerable. The citizens argued about it loudly for days on end. At last, they resolved to march to the next town and settle the matter face to face, with the loudest words they could find.

At the center of the town rose a very high tower, the pride of Quiquendone. One morning, before the march was due to begin, Van Tricasse and Niklausse climbed its long stairs together, still not speaking to each other. Below them lay the fields, the canals and the long road to the next town. At the top, however, where the heavy oxygen could not rise, the air was as ordinary as it had always been, and a strange calm came over the two old men. Gazing down on the little town, they found their quarrel absurd, and they shook hands as warmly as ever. The peace lasted exactly as long as the descent of the stairs; at the bottom, they were shouting again.

It was then that a tremendous sound rolled across the town from beyond its walls. Doctor Ox's gas house had exploded. By good fortune everyone was safe, but the building had vanished, and the pipes no longer carried a breath of oxygen into Quiquendone. Throughout the town the shouting faded, and people looked at one another in a puzzled silence, as if waking from a long and noisy sleep.

Within a few hours, the people were as slow as they had ever been, and the fever of the past weeks seemed like a half-remembered dream. Nobody felt the slightest wish to argue with the next town anymore, and so the march quietly never took place. Van Tricasse and Niklausse forgot their quarrel, if indeed they could recall it at all, and resumed their long and peaceful meetings, at which, to their deep content, nothing whatever was decided. Doctor Ox and Ygene left the town without a word and were never seen in Flanders again. As for the citizens, they never learned the true cause of those strange days. The slowest town in the world settled back into its calm, as if nothing had ever disturbed it. And the question of the street lamps, naturally, returned to its honored place on the long list of matters still under discussion.
