# The Bamboo Cutter's Daughter

Summary: An old bamboo cutter finds a tiny shining girl, and soon many great men wish to marry her.

Glossed key words (A1/A2): bamboo, moon, suitors, robe

## Level A1

An old man cut bamboo for his work. One morning, a bamboo was full of light. He cut it open. In it sat a very small girl. She could sit in his hand. He carried her home to his wife.

After this, he often found gold in bamboo. The girl grew very fast. In three months, she was a young woman. No woman in the land was so beautiful. Her name was Kaguya.

Five great men wanted to marry Kaguya. But Kaguya did not want to marry. So she asked for very special things. One man brought a robe. "No fire can burn it," he said. Kaguya put it in a fire. It burned at once. No man brought the real thing.

Then the Emperor came to see Kaguya. He loved her at once. "Come with me," said the Emperor. But Kaguya did not want to go. They wrote letters for three years.

Later, Kaguya looked at the moon every night. She often cried. "What is it?" asked the old man. "I come from the moon," said Kaguya. "My people want me back. They come for me at the full moon."

The old man asked the Emperor for help. He sent many men to the house. The old man put Kaguya in a room. At night, a great light came. Moon people came down to the house. The Emperor's men could not move.

Kaguya gave her robe to her old parents. "Keep it and remember me," she said. She wrote a letter to the Emperor. She left him a drink from the moon. With it, a person lives forever. The moon people put a robe on her. At once, her heart changed. She went up to the moon with them.

Her old parents cried for many days. Without her, he did not want the drink. His men took the drink up Mount Fuji. There, they burned it. Smoke still goes up from Mount Fuji today.

## Level A2

An old man cut bamboo and made things from it. One morning, he saw a bamboo full of light. He cut it open and looked inside. There sat a very small, beautiful girl. He put her in his hand and carried her home.

The old man and his wife looked after her like a daughter. From then on, he often found gold in the bamboo. Soon the family had a lot of money. The girl grew very quickly. After three months, she was a young woman. Her name was Kaguya.

Many men wanted to marry Kaguya. Five great men came again and again. Kaguya did not want to marry, so she had a plan. "Each of you must bring me one special thing," said Kaguya. "Then I can marry the man with the real thing."

The first man brought an old bowl, but it had no light. The second man asked workers to make a gold tree branch. But the workers came to the house for their money. The branch was not real, and now everyone knew. Another man brought a robe. "No fire can burn this robe," he said. Kaguya put it into a fire, and it burned at once. One more man went to sea, but a storm sent him back. The last man also came back with nothing.

Then the Emperor heard about Kaguya. He visited the house and loved her at once. "Come to my home with me," said the Emperor. But Kaguya did not want to go. Suddenly she became like a shadow, and he could not see her. "Come back, and I promise not to take you," said the Emperor. Then Kaguya came back. For three years, they wrote letters to each other.

Later, Kaguya began to look at the moon every night. She often cried. "What is wrong, my child?" asked the old man. "I am not a person of this world," said Kaguya. "I come from the moon. My people want me back. They come for me on the night of the full moon."

The old man asked the Emperor for help. The Emperor sent two thousand men to the house. The old man put Kaguya in a room and closed the door. At night, a great light came. People from the moon came down to the house. The Emperor's men could not move at all. All the doors opened by themselves, and Kaguya walked out.

Kaguya wrote a letter to her old parents. "Please keep my robe and remember me," she wrote. The moon people brought a special drink. With this drink, a person lives forever. Kaguya left some of it with a letter for the Emperor. Then a moon person put a robe from the moon on her. At once, her heart changed, and she forgot this world. She went up to the moon with the moon people.

The Emperor read her letter. "I do not want to live forever without her," he said. His men took the letter and the drink up Mount Fuji. There they burned them. Even today, smoke goes up from Mount Fuji.

## Level B1

Long ago, an old man lived near a bamboo forest with his wife. Every day he cut bamboo and made useful things from it. One morning, he noticed a stalk of bamboo that was glowing with light. He cut it open and found a tiny girl inside, only about three inches tall. The old man carried her home in his hand.

The old couple decided to raise the girl as their own daughter. After that, the old man often found gold hidden inside the bamboo. Slowly, the family became rich. The girl grew at an amazing speed, and in three months she was a young woman. No one in the land was as beautiful as she was. A wise man gave her the name Kaguya.

Many men came to see her, but most of them soon gave up. Five great lords kept coming, through the cold winter and the hot summer. The old man begged Kaguya to choose one of them as her husband. "I will marry the man who brings me what I ask for," said Kaguya. Then she gave each suitor a task that was almost impossible.

The first had to bring a holy stone bowl from a faraway land. He only took an old bowl from a mountain temple, and it did not shine. The second had to bring a branch from a magic tree with silver roots. He secretly paid workers to make one. But then they came to the house and asked for their money. So everyone learned that the branch was a fake.

The third had to bring a robe of fire-rat fur, which no flame could burn. He bought one from a trader, but when Kaguya put it into a fire, it burned. The fourth had to bring a jewel from a dragon's neck. He sailed out to sea, but a terrible storm drove him back, and he gave up. The fifth had to bring a special shell from a swallow's nest. His men pulled him up to the nest in a basket. But the thing in his hand was not a shell.

Soon the Emperor himself heard about Kaguya. He sent a court lady to see her, but Kaguya refused to meet her. So the Emperor came to the house himself during a royal hunt. He saw Kaguya and loved her from the first moment. When he tried to take her to his palace, she suddenly turned into a shadow. "Come back, and I will not force you," said the Emperor. At once, Kaguya returned to her true form. For three years after that, they wrote letters and poems to each other.

In the spring of the third year, Kaguya began to gaze at the moon every night. She grew sadder and sadder and often cried. At last, in the eighth month, she told her parents the truth. "I am not a person of this world," said Kaguya. "I come from the moon, and my people sent me down here for a short time. On the fifteenth night of this month, under the full moon, they will come for me." "No one will take my daughter away!" cried the old man.

The old man asked the Emperor for help, and the Emperor sent two thousand guards. Some of them stood on the roof, and the others stood around the walls. Inside, the old man locked Kaguya in a back room, and his wife held her tight.

At midnight, a light brighter than noon suddenly filled the sky. People from the moon came down on a cloud and stood in the air. The guards stared up at them, but they could not move at all. The doors opened by themselves, and Kaguya walked out. The moon people brought a flying carriage, a robe of feathers and a jar of medicine. This medicine could make a person live forever.

Kaguya wrote a letter to her parents. "Please keep the robe that I leave here, and remember me," she wrote. "On nights when the moon is bright, look up at me." She drank a little of the medicine. Then she wrote to the Emperor too. She gave the letter and the rest of the medicine to the captain of the guards. Finally, a moon person put the robe of feathers on her. At once, all her sadness and her love for this world were gone. She got into the carriage and rose into the sky with a hundred moon people.

The old couple cried for days. The captain took the letter and the medicine to the Emperor. The Emperor did not want to live forever in a world without her. He asked which mountain was closest to heaven, and his advisers named Mount Fuji. His men carried the letter and the medicine to the top and burned them there. Even today, smoke still rises from Mount Fuji into the clouds.

## Level B2

Long ago, an old man lived at the edge of a bamboo grove with his wife. He made his living by cutting bamboo and turning it into all kinds of useful things. One morning, he noticed that one of the tall green stalks was shining from within. Puzzled, he cut it open, and inside sat a tiny girl, no more than three inches tall. He carried her home to his wife in the palm of his hand.

The couple decided to raise the girl as their own, and at first they kept her in a little basket. From then on, the old man often found gold hidden inside the hollow stalks he cut. The family slowly grew rich. Within three months the girl had become a young woman, and no one in the land could match her beauty. Her presence filled the house with light, and simply looking at her made sad people feel better. A wise man was invited to name her, and he chose Kaguya, a name full of light.

News of her beauty spread far and wide, and men gathered around the fence, hoping to catch a glimpse of her. Most of them soon drifted away, but five noblemen kept coming, through winter snow and summer heat. "I am old, and I may not live much longer," the old man told Kaguya. "Please choose one of these men as your husband, so that someone will look after you." Kaguya had no wish to marry, but she did not want to hurt the old man's feelings. "I will marry whichever of them can bring me the thing I ask for," she said. Then she set each suitor a task that was close to impossible.

The first was to bring the holy stone bowl of a great teacher from a distant land. He never left the country, and after three years he simply brought her a dark old bowl from a mountain temple. She looked inside it for a gleam of holy light, but there was none, and she sent it back. The second was to fetch a branch from a magic tree with silver roots, a golden trunk and white jewels for fruit. He pretended to sail away, then secretly hired craftsmen to build a perfect copy of the branch. Even the old man was convinced, and Kaguya grew pale with worry. But at that very moment, the craftsmen arrived at the gate, demanding to be paid, and the trick was revealed.

The third was to bring a robe made from the fur of the fire rat, which could not be burned. The suitor, a rich minister, paid a merchant across the sea a huge sum for one. "If it is real, the fire will not touch it," said Kaguya. She had it thrown into the flames, and it burned up at once.

The fourth was to bring a jewel of five colors from a dragon's neck. This suitor set sail himself, but a fierce storm struck his ship, with thunder crashing and waves rising like mountains. Sure that the dragon had sent the storm, he begged it for forgiveness, and when the sea grew calm, he gave up. The fifth was to find a rare shell that swallows were said to keep in their nests. He had himself pulled up in a basket to a nest high under a roof. He closed his hand around something, but when he looked, it was not a shell at all.

Word of all this reached the Emperor, who sent a lady of his court to see Kaguya. Kaguya refused to meet her, and nothing her mother said could change her mind. So the Emperor arranged a royal hunt near the old man's home and, during it, slipped into the house. He saw Kaguya and loved her at first sight. But when he tried to take her to his palace, she suddenly turned into a shadow and vanished. "Come back, and I promise I will not force you," said the Emperor. Only then did Kaguya appear again in her true form. For the next three years, the two of them exchanged letters and poems.

In the spring of the third year, Kaguya began to sit by the window each night and stare at the moon. As the weeks passed, she grew more and more unhappy, and by summer she often wept. At last, in the eighth month, she told her parents the truth. "I am not a person of this world," she said. "I come from the city of the moon, and I was sent down to you for only a short time. On the fifteenth night of this month, when the moon is full, my people will come to take me back." The old man cried out that no one would take his daughter from him.

He went straight to the Emperor, who sent two thousand guards to protect the house. A thousand of them stood on the roof, and a thousand more surrounded the walls. Inside, the old man locked Kaguya in a back room, while his wife held her close. Kaguya told the old man gently that no lock could keep her people away.

At midnight, the house was suddenly bathed in a light brighter than noon. Beings from the moon came down on a cloud and stood in the air, a little above the ground. The guards stared up at them, but their arms and legs felt heavy, and they could not move. At that moment, every locked door in the house swung open by itself, and Kaguya walked out.

The moon people had brought a flying carriage, a robe of feathers and a jar holding the medicine of eternal life. Before leaving, Kaguya wrote a letter to her parents. "Please keep the robe I am leaving behind, and remember me by it," she wrote. "On nights when the moon is out, look up at me." She tasted a little of the medicine and wrote a final letter to the Emperor. Then she handed the letter and the jar to the captain of the guards. A moon being placed the robe of feathers around her shoulders, and all her sorrow faded away at once. She stepped into the carriage and rose into the sky with a hundred moon people.

The old couple wept until they fell ill, and nothing could comfort them. The captain brought the letter and the jar to the Emperor, and he read the letter in silence. "What use is eternal life to me, if I can never see her again?" said the Emperor. He asked his ministers which mountain was closest to heaven, and they named Mount Fuji. His men carried the letter and the jar to the top and set both on fire. It is said that smoke still rises from Mount Fuji, up into the clouds, to this day.

## Level C1

In an age long past, at the edge of a great bamboo grove, there lived an old man whose whole life was bound up with that tall, rustling plant. One morning he caught sight of a single stalk that glowed from within, as though someone had hidden a lantern inside it. Wondering, he cut it open, and there in the hollow sat a tiny girl no more than three inches tall, lovelier than anything he had ever seen. He lifted her onto his palm and carried her home to his wife, telling himself that a child found in the very bamboo he tended must have been meant for him.

The old woman took the girl into her care with delight, and because she was so small, the couple kept her at first in a little basket. From that day on, a strange good fortune followed the old man, and again and again he found gold packed inside the hollow joints of the stalks he cut. The child, meanwhile, grew like a young plant after spring rain, and within three months she had become a young woman of full height. Her beauty was of a kind no one had seen before, and it was said that anyone weighed down by sorrow forgot it merely by looking at her. A wise man was asked to name her, and he chose Kaguya, a name full of shining light, after which the family held a feast that went on for three days.

It was not long before the whole country was talking of the bamboo cutter's daughter, and men crowded around the fence, peering through the gaps in the hope of a glimpse. One by one most of them gave up, yet five noblemen of high rank refused to be discouraged, coming back through the snows of winter and the burning heat of summer. "I am past seventy, and I cannot know how many years remain to me," the old man told Kaguya at last. "In this world a woman marries, and I would rest easier knowing that one of these men was there to care for you." Kaguya answered that she could not give herself to a man without knowing the depth of his feelings, and she promised to marry whichever suitor could bring her what she asked.

To the first suitor, a prince, she gave the task of finding the stone begging bowl of a holy teacher who had lived long ago in a distant land. He let three years pass without ever setting out, then fetched a bowl blackened with age from a mountain temple and presented it as the real thing. Kaguya looked inside it for the faintest glimmer of holy light, found none, and sent it straight back. The second, also a prince, was to bring a branch from a magic tree on a mountain in the eastern sea. That tree was said to have silver roots, a golden trunk and white jewels for fruit. Far more cunning than the first, he announced that he was sailing away, then hid with a team of craftsmen who made him a perfect copy. When he brought it to the house with a long tale of storms and strange creatures, even the old man was convinced, and Kaguya's heart sank. At that very moment, however, the craftsmen appeared at the gate demanding the wages they had never been paid, and the whole deception collapsed in an instant.

The third suitor, a wealthy minister, was to obtain a robe woven from the fur of the fire rat, which no flame could harm. He paid a merchant across the sea a fortune for one, and when it arrived, its fur shimmering with a deep blue light, he felt certain of success. "If it is truly what it seems, the fire will not touch it," said Kaguya, and she had it laid upon the flames. It burned away to nothing in a moment, and so did the minister's hopes.

The fourth suitor, a great lord, was asked for a jewel of five colors from a dragon's neck. When his servants took the supplies he gave them and scattered to their homes, he set out to sea himself. There a storm caught his ship, so fierce that the sky turned black, and, convinced that the dragon itself had raised it, he prayed to it for mercy. When at last he reached the shore again, he wanted nothing more to do with dragons, jewels or Kaguya. The fifth was to find the rare shell that swallows were said to keep in their nests. Determined to seize the prize himself, he had his men pull him up in a basket to a nest under the high roof of a palace kitchen. But when he opened the hand he had closed on his treasure, he found only a scrap of old, dry dirt.

By now the story had reached the palace, and the Emperor sent one of his court ladies to see whether the girl was as beautiful as people claimed. Kaguya would not come out to meet her, no matter how her mother pleaded. At last the Emperor arranged a royal hunt near her home and slipped into the house, where he found her sitting in a room full of light. He loved her the instant he saw her, but when he tried to lead her away to his palace, she turned into a shadow and was gone. Only after he swore that he would not take her by force did she return to her true form. For the next three years he wrote to her often, and she answered his letters and poems with a warmth she showed to no one else.

In the spring of the third year, Kaguya began to sit near the edge of the house in the evenings, gazing at the moon with a sadness she could not hide. In the eighth month, as the moon grew toward its fullness, she could no longer hide her tears, and the old man begged her to tell him what was troubling her. "I am not of this world," she said at last. "I come from the city of the moon, and I was sent down to live among you for a short while. On the fifteenth night of this month my people will come for me, and it breaks my heart to leave the two of you." The old man cried out that no one would take her from him, not even the people of heaven.

He hurried to the Emperor, who at once sent two thousand guards under a trusted captain. On the fifteenth night, a thousand of them took up positions on the walls and another thousand on the roof. Inside, the old man shut Kaguya in the innermost room, while the old woman held her tightly in her arms.

Toward midnight, a light ten times brighter than the full moon spread over the house, and the night became brighter than noon. Down from the sky, riding on clouds, came the people of the moon, and they stopped in the air a little above the ground. The guards looked up at them, but a strange weakness came over every one of them, and they could only stand and stare as though caught in a dream. One of the moon people, who seemed to be their king, called the old man out and told him that Kaguya's time on earth had come to an end. Even as the old man protested, the locked doors swung open of their own accord, and Kaguya walked out into the light.

The moon people had brought a flying carriage, a robe of feathers, and a small jar holding the medicine of eternal life. Kaguya asked them to wait, and with tears in her eyes she wrote a letter to her parents. "I never wished to leave you," she wrote, "and it grieves me that I cannot care for you in your old age. Keep the robe I am leaving behind and remember me by it, and on nights when the moon is out, look up at me." She tasted only a little of the medicine, then wrote a last letter to the Emperor and handed it, together with the jar, to the captain. Finally, the robe of feathers was laid around her shoulders, and at that moment all the sorrow she had felt for her earthly family slipped from her heart. She stepped into the carriage, and with a hundred moon people around her, she rose into the sky and was gone.

The old couple wept until they fell ill. When the captain brought the letter and the jar to the palace, the Emperor read Kaguya's words again and again and no longer called for music at court. "Of what use is eternal life to me, when I shall never see her again?" he asked. Told that Mount Fuji stood nearest to heaven, he sent a large company of men to its summit with the letter and the jar. There, at his command, they set both alight, and it is said that smoke still rises from the top of the mountain into the clouds, even to this day.
